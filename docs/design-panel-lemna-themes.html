Lemna themes design options

The user, 2026-10-31: "I want to redesign Lemna a bit. I like the "nerd" mode it has at the moment, but I feel like it can lean a bit more into the mindfulness side of things. I like the rain animation but the other ones are not really great. I want to add different themes that really change the way the app looks. I want to add a new look and sound now that leans a lot more into the mindfulness side of things, so having ambient sounds like winchimes and light rain drops as ambient animations as well as a user interface theme that is much more minimalism and also typing sounds that lean more into that theme. Make a thorough analysis what the best way is to facilitate these changes and how it would look in the end. Make a panel for me to decide the design aspects and add the animation and look previews so I can judge them properly. Keep in mind that we in the future might want to add other themes and ambients as well."

Ten decisions: how a theme works and is declared (1–2), the mindful look Still (a placeholder name, look §5.1), its type and motion (3–5), its ambience, sound bed and click pack (6–8), what goes (9) and the order of work (10). Everything comes from three notes in research_notes/Lemna themes/: architecture.md (the code at pass 20), look.md and sound.md; the cards cite them as architecture, look and sound with their sections. recommended is the proposed pick, ask a pick that amends a DESIGN decision (named on the tag), no advised against by the evidence.

The previews are live and drawn at real size, 1 CSS px = 1 pt, as on a 2× screen; typing into any box glides its line. The sounds and the Literata face play from the results copy, which carries them in its previews/ folder; in the repository's docs/ those links do not resolve, the players stay silent and the Still boxes fall back to Charter 31 (the links to the notes resolve only there).

What holds today, and what the notes found

Today's one look is the user's "nerd mode": box #0e1013, text #d8dce3, accent #7aa7f0, Menlo 28, a 1 pt accent border, 10 pt corners, the dim the box's colour at 0.5 (DESIGN F1's T2, B1, D1).

The look lives in four places.Style (five colours and the font), about 20 literals in the app, Effects.Tuning (29 reads) and Ambience.Tuning (97 reads, with its own copy of two Style colours); five objects capture the look once at init and would not follow a change (architecture takeaway 1, §1).
Two things assume a dark look.The ambience is added light, which shows nothing on a light dim; the box's shadow is dropped on the hand-off's first frame, a pop on a light dim (architecture takeaway 2, §7).
Calm apps are quiet, not decorated.Warm paper or near-black, never pure white on black; text at 7–10:1, not 15–20:1; no border round the writing area; one accent; still backgrounds; fades of 250–600 ms and no motion of the text (look takeaway 1, §1).
Light reads a little better; a pale wash glares at night.Dark-on-light won for normal vision, by little at Lemna's size; a pale wash over a dark app brightens the screen about 7× at every summon, hence a night variant chosen by the system's appearance (look takeaway 2, §2.1).
Rain is neutral; chimes are not.Looped rain did not differ from silence during a serial task (t(17) = 1.14); tone sequences add 4–7 points of serial-recall errors whatever their level (sound takeaway 1–2, §1.2, §1.4).
Few apps bundle sound with a look.Writing apps theme colours and keep the font apart; terminals bundle more; only Calm's scenes join picture and sound; following light and dark is the norm (look §4).
Kept, under every pick.Today's look stays as the theme Lemna, equal to today exactly, its renders byte-equal (architecture §3, §8). The glide's 140 ms on easeOutQuint, the box's proportions, the menu-bar mark (L1) and the app icon stay global; the Effects ticks and ⌘K's on/off stay the user's; Reduce Motion as A1 and F1 have it (architecture §1.4, §2).
Amended by these picks.Any TH takes F1's "Not taken: T3 (a Theme submenu, later)", whose risk, "Each scheme needs its muted checked and every effect tried on it", is DC1's tests (architecture §0). MO1 amends F1 (P1, H1 and its scale of durations) for Still; AM1 and RM1 amend A1; BD1 amends K1's D1; CK1 amends K1 (pass 19a's "peaks at −6 dBFS, no gain", the ±3 % rate on every kind, the ruled-out notes for one send).
Read two ways."ambient sounds like winchimes and light rain drops as ambient animations": (a) a sound bed and animations, or (b) chimes and drops as animations only; (a) needs the bed, the costliest and riskiest part (architecture §0 Q1, takeaway 5). BD0 is reading (b).
Not on this panel, for later.Mist, a behind-window blur as a dim option, which hides the app being written into (look §2.5, §5.2); SF Pro Rounded 30 as the rounded face (look §5.1); a soft shadow once the hand-off's shadow drop is fixed (look §5.1); chimes over the rain as a bed (sound, Recommendations); a bed level control of −52, −46 and −40 LUFS (sound §2.3); a quieter bed while another app plays audio (sound §2.7).
IDs.Two letters and a digit, this panel's own; DESIGN's D1, C1, K1, H1 and the rest are named with their row (K1's D1, A1's C1).

The shared part, the same for all three: Theme.swift in LemnaCore (≈ 120 lines with two themes), Style built from a theme (≈ 20 changed), Box.restyle (≈ 50), applyTheme with its menu, its validation and a theme key (≈ 50), EdgeView (≈ 6), LineView (≈ 10), the ink through Stage (≈ 15 + 7 sites), the blend per theme (≈ 15), the renderer per font (≈ 20): about 12 files, ≈ 350 lines and ≈ 120 test lines, an estimate (architecture §2).

TH1

Theme = preset

recommended

Session › Theme ▸ sets the look and writes the sound pack, the bed and the ambience into today's keys. Sound, Effects and Ambience stay, show what is set, and can override it until the next theme switch.

  • One choice, a coherent package: "really change the way the app looks" plus "a new look and sound" asks for one; TH3 does not give it (architecture §2, why A 1).
  • The axes stay independent in storage, so a future pack, bed or scene is one item in its own menu, usable under any theme: TH3's strength, kept (why A 2).
  • Two amendments: the preset writes pack, bed and ambience, not the Effects ticks (per-user motion preferences, their Reduce Motion handling theirs); and it leaves Sound's on/off (⌘K) alone, so it needs a variant of SoundChoice.choose, which turns the sound on (architecture §2).
  • Precedent: a preset with overrides in a higher layer, as Obsidian, Typora's user CSS, VS Code's customisations and Ghostty keep them; no complaint about either model was found (look §4, what follows 1 and 5).
  • Never a bed unasked: a theme that turns a bed on says so in its item, e.g. "Mindful (rain)", here "Still (rain)" (sound §5).
  • TH1 turns into TH3 if the preset is deleted, into TH2 if only the storage keys change (why A 5).

Risk. A tweak is lost at the next theme switch; themes are switched rarely and the menus show what holds (why A 3). Every axis can be set by hand, so every scene and pack must work on every theme: a dark theme's added light on a light dim is invisible (architecture §2, §7).

TH2

Bundle with per-theme overrides

Each theme remembers its own pack, bed and ambience; switching back brings your tweaks back.

  • VS Code's per-theme customisations are the precedent "if lost tweaks are ever reported" (look §4, what follows 5).

Risk. About 4× TH1's extra code for a problem nobody has had yet; hidden state and a test matrix of axes × themes; questions TH1 never has: is ⌘K per theme, is Reduce Motion (architecture takeaway 3, §2 why A 4).

TH3

Look only

A theme changes the look alone; pack, bed and ambience stay separate choices, as OmmWriter keeps them (look §1.1).

  • Nothing beyond the shared part (architecture §2).

Risk. The mindful set takes three menus, and a theme alone does not "really change" the app; mismatches such as the films' Rain on a paper look (architecture §2).

DC: how a theme is declared

Where the themes are written down, and what checks them.

public struct Theme: Equatable {
    public let id: String, title: String, dark: Bool
    public let palette: Palette, type: Typeface, shape: Shape, dim: Float, ink: Ink
    public let pack: String, bed: String?, ambience: Ambience.Mode?   // the preset (A)
    public static let all: [Theme] = [.lemna, .mindful]                // the first is the default
}

The sketch from architecture §3. Fields per architecture §1.4: id and title; dark (appearance, blend, shadow policy); the palette with its Increase Contrast muted; the type (font, size, an optional bundled file, monospaced); the shape (corner radius, border width and role, caret, shadow); the dim's alpha; the ambience's ink (blend, two colours, an alpha scale); optional motion; the preset.

DC1

A Swift literal per theme, with tests

recommended

Each theme is a Swift value in LemnaCore, beside Effects.Tuning, Ambience.Tuning and SoundChoice.packs; a ThemeTests.swift checks them.

  • Checked at compile time: the Ambience.Mode cases and every field's type; Theme.all straight from swift test, no bundle (architecture §3).
  • The tests: ids and titles unique, the first lemna, an unknown or missing kept value read as lemna; lemna equal to today exactly (T2's colours, Menlo 28, radius 10, border 1, dim 0.5); every pack, bed and mode named exists; contrast per theme: text ≥ 7:1, caret and accent ≥ 3:1, muted under Increase Contrast ≥ 4.5:1; every scene visible on every theme's dim, one offscreen frame per theme and mode, the test that catches added light on a light dim; the font resolves to the face asked for; the preset leaves the Effects ticks and the sound's on alone (architecture §3).
  • Assets keep their own manifests: a bed is a folder with a bed.json in the shape of pack.json (name, licence, source, attribution, files) (architecture §3).

Risk. No third-party themes. Pack and bed folders are listed in three places (SoundChoice.packs, make-app.sh, the LemnaClicks exclude list), and a new one can be forgotten in any; one glob in make-app.sh removes one of them (architecture §3).

DC2

A JSON manifest in Resources

Each theme is a file in the bundle, read at launch.

  • Third-party themes become possible; no need for them shown (architecture §3).

Risk. Checked only at run time: a typo'd mode or colour is a launch-time error path; the tests need the file from the repo and a decoder to test (architecture §3).

LK1

Still, following the system's appearance

recommended

By day a warm paper box on a pale sage wash, by night a warm near-black on a deep green-black; NSApp.effectiveAppearance picks, and no manual switch until asked (look §5.1).

  • Light reads a little better: with normal vision dark-on-light won proofreading and acuity at every age (Piepenbrock et al.); light mode did better at night in a glance task, by day no difference (Dobres et al. 2017); the advantage is minimal at 28–30 pt (look §2.1).
  • Never darker than its surround: a pale box is the only one that meets EN 12464-1's task-brighter-than-surround over every app (look §2.1).
  • Why the night variant: a pale wash over a dark app raises the screen's luminance from 0.013 to 0.38, a 6.9× contrast ratio, at every summon: no flash hazard, but glare at night; Ulysses, iA Writer, VS Code, Ghostty and kitty follow the appearance (look §2.1, §4).
  • Softer contrast at no cost: reading is "very tolerant to contrast reduction" for letters of 0.25–2°, and Lemna's em is about 0.64° (Legge et al. 1987; look §2.2).
  • No border: none of the calm writing apps draws one; a tonal hairline and the warm-on-cool split let the box be found (look §2.4).
  • Smooth on paper: one 8-bit step is 1 % of luminance at code 230 against 8.8 % at code 14, so a fading ring or mist draws without bands on a pale wash (look §2.1).

Risk. All of architecture §7's light-only work: the ambience blended over (a second pipeline keyed by blend) with alphas per theme, .aqua on the window and the panel, no shadow until the hand-off's drop is fixed, the theme's own Increase Contrast pair. It is invisible until a light theme exists, hence the light stub in pass 1 (architecture §7, §8). The dims assume sRGB-encoded compositing, not checked against WindowServer (look §7).

TY1

Literata 30, bundled under the OFL

recommended

Literata, the e-book face commissioned for Google Play Books (2015), at 30 pt, its optical size at the size, regular; Charter 31 when it is missing (look §1.4, §5.1).

  • 30 pt keeps Menlo 28's x-height (15.1 against 15.3 pt); the box at 4 em becomes 120 pt (look §5.1).
  • Bundled: OFL-1.1, 0.96 MB variable (a static 400 instance is smaller), in Contents/Resources/Fonts with its OFL.txt and a THIRD_PARTY.md line, registered with CTFontManagerRegisterFontsForURL or ATSApplicationFontsPath (architecture §6).
  • Type is character, not legibility: serifs make no measurable difference (Arditi & Cho 2005), the fastest font differs per reader (Wallace et al. 2022), rounder letters felt more pleasant and read a little faster (Medved et al. 2023) (look §2.3).
  • About 40 % more text per width than Menlo (12 against 16.9 pt a character), and each glide step varies with the glyph, "which is how type moves on paper" (look §2.3).
  • iA Writer's fonts are out: OFL, but their makers ask text editors, "minimalist text editors" among them, not to bundle them (look §2.3).

Risk. Menlo is assumed in the code: Drift's speed of one cell, Line.tail's 200 characters (a narrower face spans about a quarter less), CoreTests' one advance, the edge note's 12 pt tuned at 28 pt; the ambience's renderer is made once from the first font and must be keyed by font (≈ 20 lines) (architecture §6). Rain's grid should keep a monospaced face whatever the line uses (look §2.3). Not yet judged in the box by eye against Menlo 28 (look §7).

MO1

Fades only

recommendedask: amends F1's P1, H1

Under Still the box opens and closes by fade, no scale; the glide keeps its 140 ms; the hand-off becomes a fade and one ring on the water.

  • The calm register is the HIG's Reduce Motion register applied by taste: "Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades"; Bartram ranks blink < linear < zoom < travel in distraction (look §2.7).
  • The values: open a 300 ms fade, close a 200 ms fade, on F1's curve; glyph fade 80 ms (140 to try); the ` ¶ `'s 200 ms unchanged (look §5.1).
  • The glide stays 140 ms: slower text reads as lag, and slower is not calmer at the fixation point (look §2.7).
  • The hand-off: "the text dropped into the water", a discrete, self-caused event at the end of a session, not in a planning pause (look §2.7).

Ask: against F1. F1 kept P1 and H1 in pass 18 as "the one bold moment, the one place that spends it"; Still trades it for a quiet one, the Lemna theme keeps both. The ring's 1.2 s lies outside F1's one scale of durations (80, 140, 200, 300 ms), to which architecture §3's test holds a theme's motion. Open in the notes: when the wash lifts, since a ring "across the wash" needs it for 1.2 s while the paste lands under it at about 130 ms; and the ring needs the dim's Metal layer at every hand-off, Ambience on or off [inference].

  • F1 as decided, "the one bold moment"; no new code, no new durations.

AM1

Still water

recommendedask: amends A1

Raindrop rings on the dim: a drop per word typed and rare drops at rest; each drop's rings rise softly, spread, fade with radius and time, and add where they cross.

Drops:
  • Lemna's own habitat, duckweed on still water, and the cheapest of the mindful set to draw well (look takeaway 7, §3.2).
  • Per word, not per key: a drop per word comes at about 0.7–0.9 Hz at 40–55 words a minute, discrete, near Bartram's ~1 Hz, and it tells of the writing (calm technology); a drop per key is the 3–8 Hz onset train F0 rejected for the box. Rest only is a bed that ignores the keys: pure mood (look §3.4).
  • The opposite of Rain's pass 19b, on purpose: no "more intense when writing", so the two themes feel different in kind (look §3.4).
  • Restoration is for pauses: nature views helped between blocks of a task, not beside it (Lee et al. 2015; modest overall, Ohly et al. 2016) (look §3.1).
  • On the sprite pipeline: one new ring shape (a Gaussian across the stroke), shader +12 lines, Sprite.Kind +1, the scene ≈ 120 lines, tuning ≈ 10, 2–3 tests: about a day; rings that cross add and read as interference, where true interference needs a full-screen pass every frame (+200–300 lines, power at rest) (architecture §5).

Ask: against A1. A1's rule for every mode is "typing feeds and silence starves"; drops at rest join Rain's exception (pass 19b, I1). Rings are a looming-like expansion, so onsets stay soft and rings small, ≤ 160 pt (look §3.3). Return counts as a word's end, which puts one drop in the planning pause where F0's (g) objected to a ripple at the box (look §2.7, §5.3). Every alpha is a starting value to judge by eye on a light and a dark app (look §5.3, §7).

AM2

ask: amends A1

Five rods hang from the top edge and sway, nudged at each Return.

  • Rods as rect sprites turned about a pivot, strings as thin rects, a damped pendulum per rod: the scene ≈ 110 lines, tuning ≈ 10, 2 tests (architecture §5).

Risk. A continuous oscillation near the 0.2 Hz visionOS warns about, figurative, and its sound is the changing-state kind K1 ruled out: "later, if the chime bed is built" (look §3.3).

AM3

Breathing glow

no

The dim brightens and darkens at a breath's pace.

  • Cheapest, on Core Animation: the shade's opacity or a CAGradientLayer, ≈ 30 lines in Box, nothing on the scene thread (architecture §5).

No. A pacer beside a task competes with it: adherence fell to 27.5 % under a hard task; Apple's Breathe is the foreground task and is never shown beside one (look §3.3; visuals.md §2.4, §4.1). F0 rejected a breathing background, "a blink when seen, a pacer when strong" (DESIGN F0).

recommendedask: amends K1's D1

BD3

Wind chimes as a bed

no

Metal chimes in a breeze under the writing.

  • Chris W 432813, CC0: 47 onsets a minute, ringing 2.4 s to −30 dB, 27 tonal peaks from 0.7 to 4.6 kHz (sound §3.2).

No. A chime is the changing-state sound of the irrelevant-sound literature: tone sequences add 4–7 percentage points of serial-recall errors, level does not reduce it and quiet does not make it safe; nobody has tested one strike every few seconds (sound takeaway 2, §1.4–1.5); K1 ruled out notes. If wanted anyway: rare strikes over the rain, at most 3 a minute, at least 8 s apart, never timed to the writer's pauses (sound, Recommendations).

CK1

Stone

recommendedask: amends K1

Unpitched stone-on-wood letters, a fingertip on wood for Space, a low slit drum for Return and one soft hand chime as the send: one material family, no melody.

  • What survives a fast typist is what K1 already found: short, dark-to-mid, low pitch salience; OmmWriter's xylophone keystroke was "absolutely NOT for fast typing" (sound §4.1).
  • Letters at the Keychron's loudness: 26–30 ms to −30 dB, 1.2–1.9 kHz, unpitched (sound §4.3).
  • One tone, at the end: the send is tonal but one fixed note after the words are written, not a sequence heard while holding verbal material; unlike the Return, so a Copy (which plays the Return) is never taken for a send (sound §4.4).
  • Engine: a per-kind pitch jitter in pack.json, 0 for pitched kinds, since ±3 % is a semitone of random pitch on a tonal tap, a melody by construction; sends levelled by loudness, not peak (sound §4.2, Recommendations).

Ask: against K1. Pass 19a's "Peaks at −6 dBFS as every click, no gain": the chime at −6 dBFS is about 17 LU louder than the Keychron send, so it peaks near −20 dBFS; the ±3 % rate on every kind becomes per kind; and K1's ruled-out notes admit one note, the send (sound §4.2, §4.4). The fingertip taps are very dark: check on the built-in speakers (sound §4.3). Nothing was listened to (sound §7).
