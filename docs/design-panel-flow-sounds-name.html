Design panel · a separate app · round 2

Flow: sounds, visuals, name

The click works; the question was what to add beside it. The evidence says: two more CC0 packs, no ambient sound, no visual effects, and a name. Ten decisions: recommended is the proposed pick, ask a matter of taste, no ruled out by evidence; the argument is in three notes of 2026-10-28.

What the research changed

Four findings; the rest of the panel follows from them.

Open 1 was wrong.Freesound has CC0 typewriter recordings with one file per key class: moodyfingers' Silver Typewriter Pack.
Ambient sound: lean no.Noise slightly harms writers without ADHD (g −0.21); speech in a bed lowers writing fluency.
Visual ambience: none.No candidate has evidence of benefit; four have evidence of cost; nobody reported a lack.
Name: Hypha.A filament that grows only at its tip; Greek "web", from "to weave".
1

A: sound packs

What ships beside today's pack; several can be picked. Short, dark strikes survive 10–14 keys/s; long, bright ones pile up.

A0

Keychron K10, as today

stays

The CC0 linear pack: 12 cuts, 102 KB, dark and short.

Open. More letter takes and a second Space and Return from the uploader's 61–66 WPM runs; originals instead of the lossy previews.

A1

Manual typewriter

recommended

Silver Typewriter Pack, CC0, one machine: keystroke, space bar, shift, carriage-return lever.

  • Cut to 90–120 ms, high shelf −6 dB above 4–5 kHz: raw strikes ring 155 ms with 81 % of their energy above 5 kHz.
  • Backspace: a keystroke at −6 dB, shortened, rate 0.9; Return: the lever, rare enough not to pile up.
  • Capitals +1–2 dB and −3 % rate, no Shift sound (platen); ≈ 0.3 MB.

Risk. Unheard; licence read on 3 of its 9 files (each page counts). Even shelved it stays brighter than the Keychron; if too bright, nvmbky's distant machine.

A2

Soft laptop

recommended

MacBook Air M2 single keys, CC0, today's uploader and recorder; Enter and Delete from the FUJITSU set.

  • Membrane keys fall 30 dB in 38 ms: the least intrusive family.

Risk. On a MacBook it doubles the key under your fingers, and any lag becomes a flam; try it there.

A3

Electric typewriter

ask

magedu's electric set, CC BY 4.0: typing, space bar, Return, power on and off.

Risk. A credit line in THIRD_PARTY.md. Electric machines hum: the CC0 IBM Selectric II run sits on a −55 dBFS floor, and per-key cuts gate it on and off; magedu's is the one clean per-class set found, not measured.

A4

Synthesised soft tick

ask

A 1–3 ms noise burst into one or two resonators, made in the mixer: 0 bytes, no licence.

Risk. Can sound sterile, like UI; the recipe is tuned by measurement, never by ear.

A5

Musical notes, water drops

no

A note or a drop per key, as Typatone does.

No. Changing pitches disrupt verbal memory more than a repeated sound (changing-state effect); at 12/s drops sound like boiling.

A6

Pencil, fountain pen

no

Paper friction per key.

No. Friction, not impact: one grain per key reads as tapping.

A7

Clicky switches as default

no

MX Blue or buckling spring.

No. A sharp high transient, a rattle at 12/s; the classic shared-office complaint.

A8

Mechvibes or Thock packs

no

The packs the key-sound apps ship.

No. No licence for the recordings; an open issue asks whether MIT covers them.

2

B: choosing a pack

A new setting, so the smallest that lets more than one pack be used.

P1

Sound becomes a submenu

recommended

Keychron, Typewriter, Soft, Off; ⌘K still toggles Off and the last pack.

  • One setting, where the toggle already is; ⌘K's meaning unchanged.

Risk. A setting to keep; the menu gains a level.

P2

⌘K cycles the packs

Each ⌘K steps to the next pack, then Off.

Risk. Muting takes up to three presses; the current pack is invisible until you type.

P3

No picker: chosen at build time

One pack in the bundle.

Risk. Trying a pack means a rebuild: fine for one person, not for others.

3

C: the typewriter bell

Flow has no line ends, so a bell has no natural moment.

C1

Never

recommended, weakly

No bell in any pack.

Risk. Loses the most typewriter-like sound; C2 is the harmless place for it.

C2

Once on ⌘⏎, typewriter only

ask

Ding and carriage return as the hand-off cue, after writing ends.

Risk. A bright 1 s ring in the app you return to, perhaps on a call.

C3

On Return

no

A ding at each paragraph.

No. A bright onset in the pause where the next paragraph is planned.

4

D: ambient sound

Rain, café or noise under the clicks.

D1

None

recommended

No bed; macOS's Background Sounds plays rain or noise beside Flow at no cost to the app.

  • No study shows a bed helps a typical writer; a café is out (speech).

Risk. The small benefit with ADHD; Background Sounds covers it.

D2

Brown or pink noise in code

ask

Procedural noise, ~40 lines, off by default, one level: 0 bytes, < 0.04 % CPU.

Risk. A second sound setting for a benefit shown only with ADHD; a recorded loop would double the app.

5

E: key-up sounds

A second sound as the key comes up.

U1

None

recommended

Key-down only, as now.

  • A typebar's rest is damped to be silent (DE698373C); Mechvibes' "key-up" is usually the press cut in half.
  • Keys are held 116 ms on average (Dhakal 2018): each release lands between presses.

Risk. None found.

U2

Key-up for the mechanical pack

Quiet release takes for the Keychron only; switches do make a second sound.

Risk. Doubles the event rate; the pack has no release takes, so they would be cut from its runs.

6

F: visual ambience

Anything that glows, moves or breathes while you type, beyond the glide and the fade.

F0

Nothing beyond glide and fade

recommended

The line glides and fades; nothing else glows, moves or breathes.

  • HIG: "avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently"; typing is the most frequent.
  • No lack reported that an effect would solve.

Risk. If the box ever "feels dead", the smallest answer is a still colour theme, not motion.

F1

(a) Glow under the caret

no

No. Redundant with key and click; "juice" raised liking, not performance, and only in games.

F2

(b) Breathing background

no

No. Invisible, it does nothing; visible, it is a pacer: adherence fell to 27.5 % under a hard task.

F3

(c) Rhythm trace

no

No. A progress line by another name: the one you removed.

F4

(d) Particles or ink

no

No. Every power-mode project turned shake off by default; gamification.

F5

(e) Paper texture

no

No, as ambience. Dither inside the line only if the fade bands on an 8-bit screen.

F6

(f) Colour drift

no

No. When noticed, the box looks different for no reason.

F7

(g) Ripple on Return

no

No. If ever wanted: the ¶ fading to muted over ~300 ms, not a ring.

G, H: two side findings

Optional tries, not recommendations; found while researching F.

G1

Glide stays 140 ms

recommended until tried

As picked in S1 after the spike.

Risk. The largest motion in the design: at ~240 ms between keys the line moves 58 % of the time.

G2

60–80 ms for one-character steps

try

Longer moves keep 140 ms.

Risk. The evidence is about motion you did not cause; judge by feel.

H1

Box as it is

recommended until tried

#0e1013 under a 35 % dim.

Risk. The ratio lighting standards ask for, inverted; no study of this setup.

H2

Lighter box or deeper dim

try

A lighter box, or the dim at α 0.5–0.6.

Risk. A deeper dim hides more of the app you write for.

Will fix without a pickReduce Transparency: the box's alpha 0.97 → 1. Increase Contrast: muted #5c646f, 3.2:1 on the box, → at least 4.5:1 (HIG).

I: the clipboard after a paste

Open since pass 10: the session is finished before ⌘V lands, which conflicts with P0, text never destroyed.

I1

Leave Flow's text on the clipboard

recommended

No restore. A missed ⌘V is one more ⌘V away.

  • No clipboard read either, so macOS 15.4's paste alert cannot come up.

Risk. The clipboard you had before is gone, as after any copy.

I2

Keep the journal a few seconds

Silently: a summon within them shows the text again.

Risk. A hidden rule; the next summon is not always empty.

I3

Accept the risk

As now.

Risk. The one way left to break P0.

N1

Hypha

recommended

A filament that grows only at its tip: the line itself.

  • Greek hyphē "web", from "to weave"; text is from texere, also "to weave".
  • The closest sibling of the maintainer's other apps: Greek, cell-scale, two syllables ending in -a.
  • No conflict in writing, focus or reading software.

Costs. hypha.app, .com, .dev taken; German readers may say "HÜ-fa"; mushroom and DAO namesakes on iOS.

N2

Raphe

second

The seam a diatom glides on, and the brain's main source of serotonin.

  • The most mindful reading of the three; raphe.app free.

Costs. RAY-fee or RAF-ee: people will say it differently; "median raphe" in anatomy. The note ranks it third, behind Ductus.

N3

Ductus

third

The flow of a hand's strokes; a duct carries flow one way.

Costs. Plosive, not calm; HVAC and "duck"; the cleanest conflict record.

N4

Lotic

if the filing is dead

Blocked by a 2022 LOTIC filing for app software, status unverified.

N5

Kinesin

Costs. Three syllables, not calm; a reading app of that name launches next month.

N6

Schwann

Costs. In German, a writing tool called Schwann reads as a STABILO product.

N7

Hillock

Costs. Most people hear only "small hill".

Dropped: Fovea (two Mac apps and an iOS speed reader on the same fixed-gaze idea), Calamus (iCalamus, a live Mac publishing app), Rill (a BI company), mindfulness words such as sati, mushin, zazen (held by meditation apps; naming a product after a practice invites the commodification critique), Flow itself (Flowstate, Flowwrite).
