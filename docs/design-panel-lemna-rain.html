Lemna rain and send-off design options

The user, 2026-10-30: "I want to revise the rain animation. I really like it and it is the one I'm using the most at the moment. I would really like it to be even more like the matrix rain animations. At the moment it only get's refreshed when I type and when the typing stops it's completely going away. I would like to have it ambient in the background (low key, but still covering more or less the whole screen) and then it should simply get more intense when writing. Maybe take a look how the animation is actually looking in the matrix movies. One additional thing is that I would like to have a really satisfying sound when sending the text off to a app. I like the default keychron sounds, they sound well, so something in the spirit of that. Maybe the sendoff sounds should be tied to the sound pack used."

Nine decisions: six on Rain (M2 of DESIGN A1), from the films' rain measured in matrix.md, and three on a send-off sound per pack, from sendoff.md; the cards cite them by section. recommended is the proposed pick, ask a pick that amends a decision, no ruled out by evidence.

What Rain does today

From Scenes.swift (struct Rain), Ambience.Tuning and DESIGN A1 (pass 16, pass 18).

Fed by keys alone.Each glyph key drops one column where the key is across the screen (column 0 of the keyboard at a tenth of the width, the last at nine tenths), higher the higher its row (55–95 % of the screen's height); Space and Return none. A drop lives 3 s: the dim is clear 3 s after the last key and the link pauses at 4 s (linger).
The string slides.The typed glyph heads the drop in the accent at α 0.8; 4–8 letters in the text colour sit at fixed offsets above it, from 0.55, each 0.75 of the one below, so the whole string moves down a row at a time, 150–300 pt/s: 4–8 rows/s on 37 pt rows (matrix §6.1).
Large and few.rainScale 1.0: Menlo 28 in 21 × 37 pt cells, 26 rows on a 982 pt screen (matrix §6.1, §7). Trail letters a–z, each swapping 1.5 times a second; 60 Hz; on every screen's dim; at most 2,000 sprites.
What the films do instead.Glyphs fixed in a grid, lit by a head that steps down; a pale head over a coloured, linear trail 23–90 rows long; speeds 2:1 per column; every glyph changing in place; the whole frame covered (matrix takeaway 1–8).
Decided, and kept.A1: Rain is one mode of Session › Ambience ▸, Off at first (D0); Reduce Motion ticks Off and plays none; no mode touches the line; a scene fades with the dim at a close. K1: four packs; no bell (C1), no ambient bed (D1), no key-up sounds (U1). F1: the hand-off H1 starts at ⌘⏎'s key-down and ends at 260 ms; Copy's glyphs rise (C1).
Amended by these picks.I1 or I2 amends A1's "typing feeds and silence starves", its 4 s end and the paused link (R1). C2 amends A1's C1 (head in the accent, trail in the text colour). S1 or S2 adds a sound kind to K1; U1 is untouched.
IDs.This panel's own: DESIGN.md's K1, S1, T1, P1, E1, I1 and W2 are other decisions. C1 here is A1's C1, today's colours.
1

G: the rain's motion

How a drop moves. The demo plays the recommended picks of sections 1–4 at real size; nothing else on this page moves.

G1

Fixed grid, light moves

recommended

The glyphs stay put; the light moves. A head steps down a column a cell at a time and lights the glyph it reaches, which then dims in place.

  • Grid: 14 pt glyphs (rainScale 0.5) at an 18.5 pt square pitch, 81 × 53 cells on a 1512 × 982 pt screen; Rezmason's 80 × 50 (matrix §6.2).
  • Evidence: Animal Logic made "certain characters ... appear to repeat or 'stick' in the same place"; Rezmason: "The 2D glyphs are in a fixed grid and don't move"; jwz: "definitely scrolled a character at a time, not a pixel at a time" (matrix takeaway 1–2, §2).
  • Trail: linear in place, the classic 2003 look (0.6 → 0 on Rezmason's palette, matrix §1.3); here from the trail's top α to 0 over trail ÷ speed seconds, each cell fading smoothly while the head jumps.
  • Speeds constant per column, about 2:1 between columns; drops in a column share it and "can never collide" (matrix §2).
  • Every lit glyph changes in place, 0.3/s idle to 1.5/s typing, an instant swap (tte 0.3/s, Rezmason 1.8/s; matrix §1.3, §6.2). A drop ends when its trail has left the screen, not at 3 s.

Risk. A new scene, not a tweak: a cell array of 4,293 cells on 1512 × 982 pt, 10,764 on 2560 × 1440 pt, where typing passes the 2,000-sprite cap (≈ 2,050–2,200) and the dimmest trail cells go first (matrix §6.2). The films are soft ("pixel effects and blurs and glows", matrix §1.3); Lemna draws no blur, and the optional head glow of matrix §6.2 is untested.

G0

Today's sliding drops, denser

no

Keep today's drops, the trail drawn at fixed offsets above the head so the whole string travels with it, only denser and running without keys.

  • The least code: the scene as it is, its spawn and life changed.

No. It keeps the one difference that most gives it away (matrix takeaway 1), first on the list of what separates a real rain from the cheap one: "Sliding strings instead of fixed glyphs" (matrix §4, item 1). The ask is "even more like the matrix".

I: the idle state

What the rain does while the box is open and no key comes. Values at I = 0 of section 3's table.

I1

Ambient from the summon

recommended, the user's askask: amends A1

The rain runs from the moment the box opens until it closes, low-key, over the whole screen; typing only raises it (section 3).

  • Low-key: about 30 % of columns showing a drop, 5–10 rows/s (93–185 pt/s), trails 6–18 rows, head α 0.35, trail top α 0.20, glyphs changing 0.3/s, at 30 Hz (matrix §6.2).
  • Spread, sparse: ≈ 2.8 new drops a second and ≈ 290 sprites on a 1512 × 982 pt screen (matrix §6.2), about 7 % of its 4,293 cells (computed), against the films' 55 % (matrix §1.4).
  • 30 Hz loses nothing: the heads step a cell at a time and the films run at 24 fps (preferredFrameRateRange 24–30), half the frames (matrix §5).
  • The first frame: from a blank dim the first heads reach the bottom 5–11 s after the summon (computed: 53 rows at 5–10 rows/s); the scene can be stepped ahead at the show, undrawn, to open mid-rain, as the demo does [inference].

Ask: against A1 and F0. A1's rule for every mode is "typing feeds and silence starves": nothing new after the last key, gone 4 s after it, the display link paused (R1). F0's evidence is against continuous motion beside the text: travelling motion beside a text task is the most distracting kind measured (Bartram 2003; Maglio & Campbell 2000), and a screen-wide idle rain is that, in peripheral vision, for the whole session (matrix §5). The user's ask overrides both; A1 gets a line. The stop stays: Ambience ▸ Off and Reduce Motion, the mechanism WCAG 2.2.2 asks of moving content that "starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds" and runs beside other content (matrix §5).

Cost. The display link and WindowServer stay busy for as long as the box is open, on every screen with W1: "Every time your app updates ... content to screen, it requires the CPU, GPU, and screen to be active" (Apple's energy guide, matrix §5). The GPU's 0.39–0.58 ms a frame (A1) is not the cost, and no watt figure exists for a full-screen overlay: measure on the build with sudo powermetrics --samplers gpu_power,cpu_power -i 1000, the box open, Ambience Off against Rain idle, before it lands.

I2

Ambient after the first key

ask: amends A1

Nothing until the first key; from then on the rain never dies while the box is up.

  • A summon only to read, copy or paste moves nothing; after the first key it is I1.

Risk. The same conflict and cost as I1 once typing starts; half of "ambient in the background", and the first key starts a whole screen at once.

I0

Today's rule

Nothing without keys; nothing new after the last key, gone 4 s after it, the link paused.

  • A1 and F0 as decided; no frames, no power at rest.

Risk. What the user asked to change: "when the typing stops it's completely going away".

T1

Ramp and key drops

recommended

Both: the whole rain rises with the key rate, and each key starts a head of its own where the key is.

  • The ramp: density to 70 % of columns, speed 15–30 rows/s, trails 10–30, head α 0.85, trail 0.50, glyph change 1.5/s, 60 Hz; one rain clock at 1/3 + 2/3 I scales speeds and changes together, as Rezmason's animationSpeed (matrix §6.2).
  • Its target I* is the keys of the last 2 s ÷ 2 s ÷ 6 keys/s, at most 1; 6 keys/s is about 70 words a minute, so ordinary typing reaches 0.5–0.8 (matrix §6.2).
  • Its time: up with τ 0.25 s, down with τ 1.2 s from 0.8 s after the last key, so the pauses between words (0.2–0.5 s) do not make it pulse, and it is back near idle about 4 s after the last key, as today's linger (matrix §6.2; the figure). Precedent: Rezmason's Playdate port, its clock 0.15× → 1× at 0.07 a frame (matrix §3).
  • Key drops: every glyph key (Space and Return none) starts a head in the key's column at the key's row height, pass 16's coupling kept (column 0 at a tenth of the width); the typed glyph at the head at α 0.95, its trail in the accent from 0.55, 8–20 rows, at its column's speed so it never cuts through a background drop; where they overlap a cell takes the brighter (matrix §6.2).

Risk. Keep the ramp at 0.25 s or slower, so the screen never pulses (WCAG 2.3.1, matrix §5). I falls only in a pause of 0.8 s or more: slower typing after a fast run keeps it high (computed from the rule).

T2

Ramp only

The whole rain rises and falls with the key rate; a key starts nothing of its own.

Risk. Loses pass 16's coupling, asked for by the user ("more reactive depending on where the key is on the keyboard", A1); a key shows only through the ramp, τ 0.25 s after it.

T3

Key drops only

The idle rain stays as it is; each key adds a drop at its place on top of it.

Risk. "More intense" is then 6 key drops a second at 6 keys/s beside the idle ≈ 2.8 (matrix §6.2); the background never speeds up, lengthens or brightens.

C: colours

Which of the box's two colours the head takes. The demo's C2 / C1 buttons switch it.

C2

The films' mapping, pale head

recommendedask: amends A1's C1

Head in the text colour #d8dce3, the palest cell; trail in the accent #7aa7f0, fading in place. The films' mapping in Lemna's palette, no green.

  • The head is the palest, not only the brightest cell in every version read: Rezmason's a near-white at 2× intensity over the trail's colour, tte's #dbffdb over #92be92 → #185318, cmatrix's and neo's white and bold: "paler, not only brighter" (matrix §1.3).
  • "Heads not distinct" is item 5 of what gives a cheap rain away (matrix §4); C1 is "the reverse" of the films' (matrix takeaway 2).
  • A key's head keeps the typed glyph legible: the text colour at α 0.95 (matrix §6.2).

Risk. Amends A1's C1, which needs the user's OK (matrix §6.1). Over a light app either mapping nearly vanishes: 1.1:1 at α 0.3 against a dimmed white app (A1).

C1

Today's: accent head

Head in the accent, trail in the text colour, as A1 decided.

  • Nothing to amend; the same two colours.

Risk. A pale trail under a blue head, the reverse of the films' mapping (matrix takeaway 2).

K: the glyphs

What the cells show. Lemna's atlas holds ASCII 33–126 and Latin-1, no katakana (Ambience.Glyphs; matrix §1.2).

K1

The films' set, as far as ASCII has it

recommended

012345789Zz:."=*+-<>|_

These 22 in every cell, plus the typed glyph at a key-started head; not mirrored.

  • The overlap of the films' set (Rezmason's font: digits without 6, z, : . " = * + < > |, 33 katakana) and tte's (2 5 9 8 Z * ) : . " = + - ¦ | _, 34 katakana): what the atlas can draw (matrix §1.2).
  • "Get the right glyphs. Like the actual ones" (Rezmason); among the wrong ones "plain Latin text" (matrix §4, item 9).
  • Not mirrored: the films turned the glyphs "back to front" (Whiteley, matrix §1.1), but whether a sprite of negative width draws mirrored in Lemna's shader is UNVERIFIED (matrix §6.2); a later try.
  • Letters stay the writer's: letters in the rain could read as words beside the line [inference]; Drift is already the mode made of the text (A1).

Risk. 22 glyphs against the films' 57 (matrix §1.2), and none of the katakana that make up most of them: the closest the atlas comes.

K2

Letters, as today

abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz

The trail's glyphs stay a–z (Glyphs.letters); the head is the typed glyph.

  • Lemna's own (matrix §6.1: "a–z is Lemna's own").

Risk. "Plain Latin text" is on Rezmason's list of wrong glyphs (matrix §4, item 9); letters beside the line.

W: where

Which dims the rain plays on. The box's screen only matters with more than one.

W1

Every screen

recommended, as today

Every screen's dim rains, as Rain does today; the other modes stay on the box's screen.

  • Rain's reach today: each dim has its own layer, and Rain falls on all of them (A1, everyScreen); "covering more or less the whole screen".

Risk. With I1 every screen moves for as long as the box is open: on three screens three times W2's frames, and travelling motion at the edge of sight on the ones you are not looking at (matrix §6.3, item 3; §5).

W2

The box's screen only

The rain on the box's screen; the others keep a plain dim, as under Drift, Rings and Grid.

  • A third of the cost on three screens, and nothing moving at the edge of sight on the others (matrix §6.3, item 3); the ambience panel's own first proposal (other screens a plain dim, A1).

Risk. A change from today's Rain; with one screen W1 and W2 are the same.

S1

One per pack, from its own recordings

recommended

Each pack gets one send sample, cut from the recordings it already uses, played once at ⌘⏎'s key-down with the hand-off motion; its arrival, a key's release or a carriage's stop, lands inside the motion's 260 ms.

  • In line with the motion: "it's so much more satisfying when everything lines up nicely … pushing it back or forward by as little as ten milliseconds sometimes makes a difference" (Hugo Verweij, Apple, WWDC 2017 session 803; sendoff §1.2). Arrivals at 97–257 ms; Apple's own sent sounds peak at 220 ms (Messages) and 310 ms (Mail) (sendoff §1.1).
  • In the pack's material, as asked ("tied to the sound pack used"): the same uploaders and, for three packs, the same files (sendoff takeaway 2, §2); Apple recorded the Watch's sounds from its own materials so they seem "made by the device itself" (sendoff §1.2).
  • U1 stands: the release is part of one sample started at key-down; there is no key-up path (sendoff §5).
  • No gain: peaks at −6 dBFS like the clicks; from length alone a send lands −2.8 to +0.5 LU of its pack's Return and 4–7 LU over its letters, Electric level with both (sendoff §4). Same player: volume 0.6, ±3 % rate, which moves an arrival by 8 ms at most (sendoff §4).
  • Cost: a send kind and one file per pack.json, ≈ 133 KB beside the packs' ≈ 541 KB, one of the mixer's 16 voices for ≤ 0.45 s, a THIRD_PARTY.md row per pack (sendoff §5).

Risk. Nothing was listened to (sendoff §7): the releases are found by timing and level, the Typewriter's stop by its level after the run, and the smartphone recording's noise reduction may smear the whirr. Try by ear first; if a send is weak beside Return, --gain +2 first, the Typewriter the likeliest (sendoff §4).

S2

One send for every pack

The Keychron cut, whatever the pack.

  • One file and one licence row; the user likes the Keychron sounds.

Risk. A keyboard's Enter after a typewriter's strikes: not "tied to the sound pack used", the user's own suggestion.

S0

No send sound

⌘⏎ stays silent, as today.

  • "Silence is golden", "we definitely don't do this for every interaction" (Verweij); "You never want to play a sound just to play a sound" (Conor O'Sullivan, Google); Gmail plays none, as users report, UNVERIFIED (sendoff §1.2).

Risk. The user asked for "a really satisfying sound when sending the text off".

P: Copy ⌘⇧⏎, and a ⌘⏎ that copies

⌘⏎ copies instead of pasting with no Accessibility permission, under Secure Input, with no app behind, or from the window (done; sendoff §3).

P1

Copy plays Return; send only for a paste

recommended

Copy plays the pack's existing Return sample (paragraph), the press alone; the send plays only when ⌘⏎ pastes. A ⌘⏎ that copies instead plays Copy's sound.

  • Copy's sound per pack: Keychron and Soft the Enter press without its release; Typewriter the platen ratchet, the paper rolling up as Copy's glyphs rise (F1 C1); Electric the clutch's impact and the run's start. No new file (sendoff §3).
  • Two related sounds: Apple's two transition sounds so "you know if you're moving forward or backward"; Facebook's send and sent "sonically have the same characteristics" (sendoff §1.2, §3).
  • Chosen with the outcome: after Paste.decide, in the same handler; the ⌘V is posted blind, so "sent" means decided, not confirmed (sendoff §1.4).
  • A "sent" sound when nothing was sent is what users called "very deceiving" of Mail's swoosh on iOS 6 (MacRumors, 2012; sendoff §1.2).

Risk. Copy sounds like typing Return; the motion (the glyphs rise, the box goes) tells them apart (sendoff §3).

P2

Copy plays the send too

no

⌘⇧⏎ and a ⌘⏎ that copies play the send sound.

No. A "sent" sound for text that went only to the clipboard: the iOS 6 Mail complaint, and a fallback that sounds like success before its note is read (sendoff §1.2, §3).

P0

Copy silent

⌘⇧⏎ plays nothing, as today; a ⌘⏎ that copies plays nothing either.

Risk. With S1, the silence after ⌘⏎ is the only cue that nothing was pasted, until the note is read.

E: the Electric cut

Electric's return is a motor's: 1.3 s from the clutch to the margin stop (sendoff takeaway 2, §2.4). Matters only with S1.

E1

Spliced

recommended

The clutch's impact (0.305–0.45 s) crossfaded over 30 ms onto the run's end (1.45–1.76 s): 420 ms, the stop at ≈ 245 ms.

  • The stop lands on the motion's end, as the other packs' arrivals do (sendoff §2.4).
  • The credit line's "Changed for Lemna" gains "spliced" (CC BY 4.0).

Risk. A made sound: "a motor-driven carriage does not get shorter" (sendoff §2.4). The stop is a dull thud, 15 dB under the impact in the cut.

E2

The whole return, plain

From the clutch, 1,440 ms: the stop at ≈ 1.27 s; M max −25.5 LUFS.

  • The machine as recorded, nothing made.

Risk. Its first 600 ms are the pack's paragraph, Copy's sound under P1, so send and copy sound the same until then; the stop arrives about a second after the text did (sendoff §2.4).
