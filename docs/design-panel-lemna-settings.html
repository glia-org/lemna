Design panel · Lemna

Lemna settings design options

Six decisions on a Settings window and on changing Lemna's chords: where settings live (SP), which chords can be changed (SK), the recorder (SR), conflicts (SC), the window's look (SW), and how the chord is kept (SD). The evidence is research_notes/Lemna settings/survey.md, cited as survey with its sections: ten comparable apps and four libraries, the code at 2f237e3, and three throwaway probes on macOS 26.5 that registered chords and read the system's list without pressing a key. recommended is the proposed pick, ask a pick that amends a DESIGN decision (named on the tag), no advised against by the evidence.

Recommended: SP2 SK1 SR1 SC2 SW3 SD2. Only the summon chord needs a control a menu cannot hold, and it is the only global chord, the one that collides: ⌃⌥Space is macOS's next-input-source chord, Things' Quick Entry with Autofill and OmniFocus's quick entry. The box's chords are menu items, which macOS's App Shortcuts already rebind (probed), so one field in a small Settings… ⌘, window is enough, and the menus stay, ticks and all. The recorder is Lemna's own, since a library's weight goes to many shortcuts and Lemna registers one. The check reads the system's list and Lemna's menu, because registration reports nothing: it returned 0 even for Spotlight's ⌘Space. A taken chord stays the user's and the menu says it is taken. About 435 lines and 80 test lines; no dependency, no permission.

The previews are wireframes in HTML and CSS at 1 CSS px = 1 pt, light and dark, not captures of AppKit. SW2's Literata loads from app/Fonts in the repository and from the results copy's _files folder; the links to the notes, DESIGN.md and the other panels resolve only in the repository.

What holds today, and what the notes found

Today every setting is a menu, in the menu-bar item's and in the hidden main menu, and the summon chord is a constant: Hotkey in Summon.swift, ⌃⌥Space through Carbon's RegisterEventHotKey, a line in the system log if it fails (survey §1).

Registration reports nothing.On macOS 26.5 a plain RegisterEventHotKey returned 0 for every chord tried, Spotlight's ⌘Space and a chord another running app holds among them; Carbon hot keys are shared by design. Only a second registration in the same process failed (survey §6).
The field never sees a held chord.A chord another app or macOS holds goes to its holder: that app opens, the input source switches. Lemna's own would summon Lemna, so the field pauses it while it listens, as the libraries do (survey §4, §5).
⌃⌥Space is crowded.macOS's "Select next source in Input menu", Things' Quick Entry with Autofill, OmniFocus's quick entry. Things' own default, ⌃Space, is macOS's other input-source chord (survey §8).
macOS already rebinds Lemna's menu chords.App Shortcuts' NSUserKeyEquivalents changed Done ⌘↩ and Session › Copy ⇧⌘↩ in a probe with Lemna's hidden menus, and left Edit › Copy alone (survey §7).
Every utility checked has a window.None of the ten keeps all its settings in a menu; tabs or a sidebar from two panes up. Rectangle, the closest to Lemna, keeps a long menu of actions and puts the chord fields and behaviour in the window (survey §2).
Activation is where the windows fail.macOS 14's activate() does not promise activation; Ice turns into a regular app and activates the Dock first; SwiftUI's Settings scene does not open from a menu-bar app; focus bugs on 26 in Ice and CleanShot; Hidden Bar's ⌘W did nothing for want of a Close item (survey §3).
Secure Input silences ⌥ chords.While another app has Secure Keyboard Entry on (a password manager, Terminal's option), chords of ⌥ or ⌥⇧ alone and single keys stop firing (Rectangle #1592, KeyboardShortcuts #176); ⌃⌥Space has ⌃ and is not reported among them (survey §5).
No Globe, no media keys, no double-tap.Carbon hot keys take ⌃⌥⇧⌘ and a key: Globe is not handled (Rectangle #1605), media keys belong to the system, a modifier alone needs an event tap with Input Monitoring or a monitor with Accessibility (survey §5).
Kept, under every pick.The box's chords and how they reach the menu (BoxPanel.sendEvent); Carbon registration with no permission; the journal; W2's activation: the box never activates Lemna, a window does and hands back. Under every pick but SP1 the menus as they are.
Amended by these picks.DESIGN Open 7 closes: the chord becomes the user's, ⌃⌥Space its default. W2's "summoned by the hotkey" names that chord. SP1 alone amends TH1 ("those menus stay as they are") and Y1 (Start at Login in the menu).
Not on this panel.About Lemna in the menu-bar item's menu: the CC BY credits (Electric, Lab bed) are nowhere in the app, About sits in the hidden menu, and AppKit's standard About panel shows a bundled Credits.rtf, a fix of its own. ↵ to send: a change to B4, where ↵ ends a paragraph (section 2). A second global chord; modifier-only or double-tap chords (an event tap and Input Monitoring, as Raycast and Alfred have); Globe.
IDs.Two letters and a digit, this panel's own; DESIGN's rows are named with their IDs.
1

SP: where settings live

A window, the menus, or both, and what the menu-bar item's menu keeps.

SP1

One Settings window for everything

ask: amends TH1, Y1

Settings… ⌘, opens a window with General (the summon chord, Start at Login), Sound (clicks, bed, on/off) and Look (theme, ambience, effects). The menu-bar item's menu shrinks to Show Lemna, Settings…, Discard… and Quit.

  • One place, the common pattern: each of the ten apps surveyed has a settings window, none keeps all its settings in a menu, and all use toolbar tabs or a sidebar (survey §2).
  • Room to grow: a credits pane for the CC BY lines (Electric, Lab bed), which no part of the app shows today (survey §1).
  • No second surface: the hidden main menu keeps its submenus for ⌘K and the box's keys, but nobody sees it.

Risk: the look can no longer be judged where it shows. A theme, a bed or an ambience is seen and heard on the box and its dim. The window activates Lemna and would sit under Box.level, so the box goes first, as for Open Window; the menu opens over the box (whether the box keeps the keys meanwhile is untried, app README). The ticks go, a switch takes three steps instead of two, Done, Copy, Save… and Open Window leave the mouse's reach. ≈ 330 lines over SP2: three panes bound to SoundChoice, BedChoice, Theme, Ambience, Effects, refreshed by TH1's preset.

SP2

A window for what menus cannot do

recommended

Settings… ⌘, opens a small window holding the summon chord. Every menu stays as it is.

  • Split by the kind of input: choosing from a list stays a menu with ticks; typing a chord needs a field, which a menu cannot hold.
  • The name people look for: Settings… and ⌘, are where Mac apps keep this (survey §2); the window can take a pane later when a setting with evidence needs one.
  • A save folder needs no setting: the save panel remembers the last folder (SaveFolder), so the window holds one row.
  • Opening and closing as the window mode does: activateLemna() in, handBack() out, so the app in front before comes back (survey §3).

Risk. A window of one row looks thin, and settings sit in two places, the menus and the window. ≈ 90 lines for the window and its two menu items, besides SR's control.

SP3

No window: Summon Key…

A menu item opens a small panel with the recorder and nothing else.

  • About 20 lines less than SP2; nothing named Settings that promises more.
  • A sheet needs a parent window, which the menu-bar item does not have, and a popover from it needs Lemna active as well, so it is a small window under another name.

Risk. ⌘, finds nothing; the next setting that needs a field is another item in a 16-item menu.

SK1

The summon chord only

recommended

One field: Show Lemna. The box's chords stay as they are.

  • The only global one: it is pressed over every other app, so it is the only chord that collides with other apps and with macOS, and ⌃⌥Space does (the table, survey §8).
  • The rest are local: they act only while the box or the window has the keys, follow the Mac's conventions, and App Shortcuts covers anyone who wants another.
  • Closes DESIGN Open 7: its condition, a build going to others, holds now (the README links a release download; v0.1.0 is tagged).

Risk. Someone who wants ⌥↩ for Done has to know App Shortcuts exists.

SK2

Summon, Done, Copy and Save…

Four fields; the three hand-offs' key equivalents follow them in both menus.

  • The send key is a known preference in chat and capture tools (survey §9); here it would be ⌘↩ against another chord with a modifier, never plain ↩, which is a paragraph end (B4) and ⇧↩ a line break.

Risk. ≈ 90 lines more: three fields, checks among the four and against the Edit menu, both menus rebuilt on a change; a second way to do what App Shortcuts does, and the two can disagree (an App Shortcut overrides the item's own chord).

SK3

Every chord

no: the conventions

Esc, ⇧↩, ⌘Z and the rest, each with a field.

No. B4 takes standard macOS text editing as the spec; Esc, ⇧↩ and the moves are AppKit key bindings in LineView, not menu items, so changing them needs a key map of Lemna's own (≈ 200 lines more and a test per binding). No one asked to move them.

SR1

Lemna's own control

recommended

An NSControl in the app target and a Chord value in LemnaCore; Hotkey keeps registering with Carbon as today.

  • One chord does not need a framework: the libraries' weight is in naming, storing and registering many shortcuts and in SwiftUI views; Lemna has one, and its registration exists (Summon.swift).
  • Builds as Lemna builds: no package dependency (today only Lemna's own two, by path), no resource bundle for make-app.sh to copy, no licence line.
  • The rules and the checks are tested: Chord and SC2's verdict live in LemnaCore, so swift test covers them; only the drawing and the key capture are the app target's.
  • What to copy from the libraries: pause the own hotkey while listening (KeyboardShortcuts, Rectangle), take performKeyEquivalent too, show the letter of the current layout through UCKeyTranslate while storing the key code (KeyboardShortcuts), ⓧ and ⌫ clear, Esc cancels (all of them).

Risk. ≈ 290 lines to own (control 180, Chord 110) and the layout cases the libraries met over years: a chord recorded on one layout shows another letter on the next (KeyboardShortcuts #62, still open), CleanShot shipped layout fixes in five releases, Raycast lets the user choose physical key or character (survey §4). Owning the control means owning those reports.

SR2

sindresorhus/KeyboardShortcuts

The package's RecorderCocoa in the window and its registration in place of Hotkey; the chord stored under the package's own key.

  • Maintained, MIT: 3.1.0 of 2026-09-11, Swift 6, macOS 10.15+; Maccy ships it.
  • Does what SR1 would: refuses no-modifier and ⇧-only chords, checks the main menu and CopySymbolicHotKeys under a policy (block, warn, allow), pauses the app's chords while recording, shows the current layout's letter; Carbon registration, no permission.
  • RecorderCocoa is a plain AppKit NSSearchField; no SwiftUI needed in Lemna.

Risk. Its 17 localisations come through Bundle.module, on which a script-built app crashes away from the Mac that built it, and it reads them even to name the Space key (#231 on 26.1; the known fix copies the bundle into Contents/Resources and patches the package to look there). So make-app.sh copies a bundle and Lemna patches or forks the package; ≈ 4,700 lines arrive for one chord, Lemna's first package from outside; the chord lives as a JSON string under KeyboardShortcuts_summon beside Lemna's own keys; SD2's launch check stays Lemna's to write.

SR3

MASShortcut or ShortcutRecorder

no: older, Objective-C

One of the two Objective-C recorders.

  • MASShortcut (BSD-2, ≈ 2,240 lines) is what Rectangle ships, as its maintainer's fork; ShortcutRecorder (CC BY 4.0, ≈ 11,100 lines) also takes modifier-only chords through an event tap.

No. MASShortcut is archived (last tag 2019; its SwiftPM manifest came after it, so a revision is pinned); ShortcutRecorder's last release is of 2022, its licence asks for a credit, and its asset catalog needs actool, which the Command Line Tools lack. Both carry localisation bundles with SR2's Bundle.module problem.

Registration reports nothing.A plain registration returned 0 for every chord tried, Spotlight's and another app's included: Carbon hot keys are shared, "the same hot key can, however, be registered by multiple applications" (CarbonEvents.h). An exclusive trial agreed with the system's list for 64 of its 148 chords.
The field never sees a held chord.A chord another app registered, or one macOS holds, goes to its holder first: the other app opens, the input source switches (a third-party probe, warble #109; Alfred's and Raycast's advice on ⌘Space). Lemna's own chord would summon Lemna, so the field pauses it while it listens, as KeyboardShortcuts and Rectangle pause all theirs and Ice the edited one. Recording a chord macOS holds needs PushSymbolicHotKeyMode, which acts only with the Accessibility grant (survey §3, §5).
The system's list is public.CopySymbolicHotKeys (Carbon, not deprecated in the 26 SDK): 230 entries here, 161 enabled, key code, Carbon modifiers and the enabled flag, no names ("no way to determine which hotkey … corresponds"); 3.6 ms a call. The plist behind it and the private CGS calls add nothing a check needs (survey §5).
Another app's chord cannot be seen.Non-exclusive registrations are not listed anywhere; when one is pressed, the other app reacts. No public API lists them (Electron's maintainers: “OS X doesn't provide a public API to check”); a third-party probe found the first registrant getting the key, against the header's “sent to all registrants” (survey §5).
SC1

Register and report a failure

no: never fails

Keep the chord; if RegisterEventHotKey refuses it, say so.

No. On the probe it refused only a second registration in the same process: SC1 behaves as SC3 with an extra branch.

SC2

Rules, Lemna's menu and the system's list

recommended

Before a chord is kept, and again at each launch, three checks, the first two refusing and the third warning.

  • Rules (refuse): a letter, digit or punctuation key needs two modifiers, one of them ⌃ or ⌘ (⌃⌥L, not ⌃L or ⌘L); Space, ↩ and ⇥ take a single ⌃ or ⌘; ⌥ or ⌥⇧ alone never; only F1–F20 go without.
  • Why so strict: ⌘ with one key is every app's menu chord (a global ⌘L takes every browser's address bar), ⌃ with one a text field's and Terminal's (⌃K, ⌃C); ⌥ and ⌥⇧ type characters (⌥Space a no-break space), macOS 15.0–15.1 refused them (−9868, until 15.2) and Secure Input silences them. MASShortcut passes anything with ⌘ or ⌃, and ⌥ with Space or Esc.
  • Lemna's own (refuse): the hidden main menu's key equivalents, read from the menu, so ⇧⌘↩ or ⌃⌘F cannot become the summon chord.
  • The system (warn, keep): an enabled entry of CopySymbolicHotKeys with the same key code and modifiers, the Fn bit (0x20000) masked, since 104 of the enabled entries carry it for function and arrow keys. The common ones named from a short table (Spotlight, input sources, Character Viewer, screenshots, Mission Control); the rest "macOS uses it".
  • In LemnaCore, against a list passed in, so swift test checks it without Carbon: ≈ 50 lines, ≈ 40 test lines.

Risk. A chord another app holds passes every check; the user finds out when that app opens instead. The table of names needs a look at each macOS release.

SC3

No check

Keep whatever was typed.

Risk. ⌘C as the summon chord breaks copying in every app until it is changed back, and a chord macOS holds fails without a word, as ⌃⌥Space does today where the input-source chord is on.

SW: the window's look

Wireframes at 1 CSS px = 1 pt in HTML and CSS, not captures of AppKit. Under SP2 the window holds one row; under SP1 three panes.

SW1

System window with toolbar tabs

with SP1

NSTabViewController in its .toolbar style: a tab per pane, the window titled by the tab, system controls, the system's appearance.

  • What every surveyed utility with a window does (survey §2), so it needs no explanation; AppKit draws it and follows macOS's restyles (26's toolbars) for free.
  • Tabs pay off from three panes up: SP1's General, Sound and Look.

Risk. For SP2's one row a tab bar with one tab is chrome around nothing.

SW2

The window in Lemna's theme

no: custom controls

The theme's palette, edge and corners; Literata under Still, Menlo under Lemna.

  • It would look like the box, and the box is what people see of Lemna.

No. AppKit's pop-ups, checkboxes and buttons take an appearance (light, dark) and the system accent, not a palette or a face, so a themed window means drawing them, ≈ 120–200 lines more, and restyling it on a theme switch and on Lemna's always-dark look. A settings window is system chrome, read for a few seconds; the box's look is for writing.

SW3

System window, one page

recommended with SP2

A plain titled window, "Lemna Settings", labels right-aligned to a column of controls as macOS lays out a settings pane; system controls; the system's appearance; no tabs.

  • The least code: one view, no tab controller; SW1 is the step after if a second pane ever comes.
  • Closes with its button and ⌘W (a Close item the hidden menu lacks today; in window mode it then does what the close button does, back to the box).

Risk. Under SP1 one page would run long; take SW1 there.

SD: defaults and migration

How the chord is kept, reset and checked at launch. The first part holds under either option.

Key.hotkey in UserDefaults, a dictionary {key: 49, modifiers: 6144}: the virtual key code and Carbon's modifier mask, what RegisterEventHotKey takes and CopySymbolicHotKeys reports, so nothing converts on the way. Registered as a default at launch, as pass 32 registers the others: {49, 6144} is ⌃⌥Space (controlKey | optionKey).
Migration.None: no key exists today, so every Mac reads the registered default, today's chord. Hotkey.keyCode and carbonModifiers become that default.
Clear, reset, bad values.× or ⌫ while listening writes {}: no hotkey, Show Lemna from the menu only, without a chord. Default removes the key, so the registered default shows again. A value that does not decode, or that the rules refuse, reads as the default.
The menus follow.Show Lemna's key equivalent is built from the chord (the key's character from the current layout, Space as " ", an F-key as its function-key character), none when cleared.
Default chord.⌃⌥Space stays (Open 7, the user's). It is also macOS's next-input-source chord, which Apple lists for Macs with more than one input source (whether it is on with one was not found), and the HIG's own example of why to avoid ⌃: “The system uses Control in many systemwide features”; SD's launch check is what tells a new user so.
SD1

Fall back to the default and say so

At launch a stored chord that macOS holds (or that does not decode) is replaced by ⌃⌥Space for the session; the menu says so.

  • Something always summons Lemna from the keyboard.

Risk: a chord the user did not choose. The default may be the one they moved away from, and it is itself held where the input-source chord is on, so the fallback can land on a second dead chord. Undoing it means knowing what happened.

SD2

Keep the user's chord and say so

recommended

The stored chord is registered as it is; if SC2's check finds macOS holding it, Show Lemna loses its chord in the menu and a grey line under it says "⌃⌥Space: taken by macOS"; the field in Settings shows the same warning.

  • Nothing changes on the user's behalf. Registration is shared and costs nothing, so the chord starts working the moment the system shortcut is turned off, with no relaunch.
  • Checked when it matters: at launch and each time the menu opens, as Start at Login's tick is read from the system each time (Y1); 3.6 ms a read. The menu is where an accessory app with no window up can say anything (today a failure goes to the system log only).

Risk. Until the user opens the menu, the keyboard summons nothing and nothing says why; where the menu bar is full, the notch can hide the item itself.
