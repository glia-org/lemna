Design panel · a separate app

Flow writing tool design options

No shipping tool has a horizontal line with a fixed caret; the parts exist separately. Hiding the text removes the urge to fix, not the ability to think: typists who could not see their output corrected 9 % of their errors instead of 42 %. Twelve decisions: recommended is the proposed pick, ask a matter of taste, no ruled out by evidence; the argument is in the full report.

1

S: the surface

Where the eye rests and which way the text leaves.

S1

One line, caret fixed

recommended

The caret stays at 60 % of the width; each key glides the line left in 50–80 ms.

  • Sliding text is hard to reread, so history stays behind you.
  • Steady motion you cause does not capture attention (Abrams & Christ 2003).
  • Reduce Motion turns the glide into a jump.

Risk. An argument may need more context than one line holds; then S3.

S2

Vertical typewriter, earlier lines dimmed

The current line stays at one height; earlier lines scroll up and dim, as in iA Writer.

Risk. History stays legible, so the pull to review stays.

S3

Both, as a setting

later

S1 by default, S2 as an option.

Risk. Two layouts and every test run twice; only if S1 stalls.

2

V: the visible span

How much of the line stays readable left of the caret.

V2

The last clause, fading left

recommended

The last words at full strength; the clause before them fades out by a quarter of the width.

  • Writers look back 13 % of the time, to plan (Torrance 2016).
  • At two visible lines one reviewer felt "paranoia".
  • The fade is a per-glyph opacity: cheap in any stack.

Risk. The right length is unknown; tune it on your sessions.

V1

The word being typed, nothing else

Only the current word shows; a space clears it.

Risk. The strongest block on rereading, and no support for planning the next sentence.

V3

The sentence, previous one dimmed

The current sentence at full strength, the previous one dimmed further left.

Risk. The most planning support and the most pull to reread; close to S2.

3

B: backspace

How far a correction can reach.

B1

Within the current word only

recommended

Backspace works back to the last space; finished words are out of reach.

  • Planted errors: 88 % fixed only after the sentence ended (Quinlan 2012).
  • Hidden output: corrections fell from 42 % to 9 %.

Risk. A typo noticed after the space waits for R1 and R2.

B0

None, append-only

Only forward, as in Draft's Hemingway Mode and Smashkey's default.

Risk. A typo you feel but cannot fix becomes a repeated irritant.

B2

Unlimited, but blind

Backspace deletes into text that is no longer visible.

Risk. Deleting what you cannot see invites rereading it in your head.

B3

Strike out instead of delete

ask

Backspace strikes the last word out; review offers each struck word to keep or drop.

Risk. A new mark type in the journal and in review.

4

P: pressure

What happens when you stop typing.

P0

None; text is never destroyed

recommended

A pause is a pause: nothing fades, nothing is deleted, nothing locks.

  • Writing under threat tripled pages, cut ideas per page ~40 % (Boice).
  • Panic comes from deletion, not from hidden text.

Risk. None from the evidence; P1 is the opt-in nudge.

P1

A gentle fade, undone by typing

ask

After a pause the visible words dim slowly; the next key brings them back.

Risk. A change you did not cause draws the eye; off by default.

P2

Deletion on pause

no

The Most Dangerous Writing App deletes everything after about 5 s idle.

No. Fewer ideas, panic, and it breaks never losing text.

5

K: key sound

A click buys pleasure and rhythm, not speed; timing matters more than timbre.

K1

Clicks from the writing window only

recommended

Each key-down in Flow's window plays a preloaded click through one audio stream kept open.

  • Feels simultaneous near 19 ms; worse by 70–100 ms (Kaaresoja 2014).
  • 128-frame buffer: about 5–20 ms on built-in or wired output.
  • Bluetooth adds 126–274 ms: warn once, offer to mute.

Risk. Untested whether clicks on a real keyboard help; try on and off.

K2

System-wide clicks via an event tap

no

Clicks for every key in every app, as Thock and Klack do.

No. Needs Accessibility, and clicks outside writing annoy; Thock already does this.

K0

No sound

Only the keyboard's own sound.

Risk. Drops the one cue the brief asks for; K1's toggle reaches it.

6

W: the window

How Flow appears and what surrounds it.

W1

Its own full-screen quiet surface

recommended for v1

A hotkey or the menu-bar item opens Flow full screen; text leaves by copy or save.

  • Carbon hotkey: no permission, works in the sandbox.
  • No Accessibility, no Input Monitoring in v1.

Risk. Text still travels by hand (⌘V there); W2 removes that step.

W2

Floating panel, paste at the end

v2

A non-activating panel over the app you were in; at Done the text pastes there.

  • Paste needs Accessibility and Developer ID (X1).
  • One overlay per screen dims the rest, with no permission.

Risk. Paste fails silently in places (Secure Input, remote desktops); Flow keeps the text.

W3

Both

later

W1 for long sessions, W2 for a paragraph into mail or chat.

Risk. The likely end state; W1 first, then W2, reaches it.

7

O: output at the end of a session

Where the text goes when you are done; several can be picked.

O1

Copy, marked private

recommended, always

Clipboard with the Concealed and Transient markers, kept off Universal Clipboard; no permission.

Risk. The markers are a convention; macOS 26's clipboard history may ignore them.

O2

Save as Markdown

recommended, v1

To a folder you pick, by atomic replace; no NSDocument Versions with hidden copies.

Risk. A saved file is ordinary data: indexed, backed up, perhaps in iCloud.

O3

Paste into the previous app

v2, with W2

Hide the panel, post ⌘V, restore the old clipboard; Unicode typing as fallback.

Risk. Needs Accessibility, rules out the App Store; Flow keeps the text until confirmed.

O4

An input method

no

InputMethodKit inserts through each app's own text system, without the clipboard.

No. Must be installed and selected; input-blocking bugs on macOS 15.2.

O5

Share sheet or Services

ask

Hand the text to Notes, Mail or a Shortcut.

Risk. The text lands in the receiver's storage; not researched in depth.

8

E: never lose, and privacy

Where the text lives while you write; surviving a power loss means a key or plaintext on disk.

E2

Encrypted journal, key deleted at Done

recommended

An AES-GCM journal per session, its key in the keychain; Done deletes the key, leaving noise.

  • write() per edit survives a crash; full flush 1 s after typing.
  • Records batched into 50 ms windows: no typing rhythm leaks.
  • Needs a signed bundle with a provisioning profile (TN3137).

Residual. Local snapshots hold journal and keychain for up to 24 h.

E0

A plaintext journal, as editors keep

no

VS Code and Vim back unsaved text up in plaintext.

No. Plaintext in snapshots, backups and Spotlight, readable by any process.

E4

Key only in a helper's memory

ask

A helper process holds the key, never on disk: a per-session switch beside E2.

Risk. Power loss or logout loses the session; the helper must verify callers.

E3

E2, key wrapped by Secure Enclave

later

A stolen backup is useless on another Mac; restoring can require Touch ID.

Risk. Does not shorten the snapshot window; add if stolen backups matter.

E6

Memory only

no

Nothing on disk.

No. A crash loses everything; hidden text must never become lost text.

9

R: review and post-processing

What happens to the raw draft; several can be picked.

R1

A deterministic pass, always

recommended

Capitals after sentence ends, double spaces collapsed, lone i to I; predictable and undoable.

Risk. Cannot add missing full stops or split run-ons.

R2

Harper review, one per change

recommended, v1.5

After the session: the full text, Harper's suggestions in a list, accept or skip each; offline.

Risk. Fewer rules than LanguageTool, English only; other languages go to R3.

R3

Hand-off to Writing Tools

recommended, the AI path

Flow does no AI; Writing Tools or Shortcuts' Proofread does it outside Flow.

Risk. Caught 3 of 5 planted errors; may use Private Cloud Compute.

R4

A language model inside Flow

no, for v1

A local or cloud model returns an edit list, accepted per change.

No, for v1. Prompted models "systematically overcorrect" (Karpo 2026); test on 5–10 drafts first.

R5

Silent correction in the background

no

Fix the text after it slides away, unseen.

No. Wispr's own clean-up "can drop or rewrite words that mattered" (Wispr).

10

T: the stack

What Flow is built in; the hard requirement is correct text input, not speed.

T1

Rust + GPUI, objc2, cpal

recommended

GPUI for window, text input and 120 Hz paint; objc2 for OS hooks; cpal for clicks.

  • GPUI implements NSTextInputClient; pin it, test dead keys per bump.
  • Glyphs at 4 subpixel offsets: the line moves in 0.25 px.
  • Pre-1.0 with breaking changes; a binary of tens of MB.

Flips to T2 on a few-MB limit, an App Store edition, or a slower spike.

T2

Swift + AppKit

strong alternative

A custom NSView with Core Text, moved by Core Animation; AVAudioEngine for clicks.

Trade-off. A few-MB binary and the best App Store story; UI rewritten for other systems.

T3

winit, egui, iced; web shells

no

Other Rust UI stacks, or Tauri, Flutter, Electron.

No. winit's dead keys break on macOS, open since 2021 (winit#2040).

11

M: the shape of a session

What a session is: feedback that informs, never rewards.

M1

Free start, optional frame, quiet end

recommended

Typing starts it; optional time box and hidden intent; a soft tone, a summary, a private log.

  • Informing feedback raised motivation; expected rewards lowered it (Deci 1999).
  • No live word count: watching your own typing slows it.

Risk. None known; the log keeps totals, never keystroke timing.

M0

A bare surface, no sessions

ask

Open, type, copy: no time box, no summary, no log.

Risk. The simplest; loses the ending and the log that make it a routine.

X: distribution and other systems

How Flow ships and where it goes after the Mac.

X1

Developer ID, outside the App Store

recommended

A signed, notarized bundle with a provisioning profile: E2's keychain and W2's paste need it.

Risk. A bundle, sign and notarize step outside cargo, set up once.

X2

X1, plus copy-only App Store

later

W1, O1 and O2 only, sandboxed; App Review rejected a ⌘V-posting tool (2026).

Risk. GPUI under the sandbox not researched; later, if reach matters.

Prototype first, open questions

Cheap to settle; none settled by the research.

1 · GPUI or AppKit spike before T is finalBuild the line in both; measure key to photon, launch and binary size.
2 · Typewriter samplesNo permissive pack found; record one or buy a royalty-free pack.
3 · The 5–10 draft test, before any AIHarper, Writing Tools and local models on real drafts; count meaning changes.
4 · 120 Hz and Reduce MotionThe glide holds 120 Hz at 10+ keys a second; Reduce Motion jumps.
5 · A deleted keychain item in a snapshotRecoverable or not; decides the wording of the discard message.
6 · The nameFlow is a working name; a later decision.
7 · Stage ManagerPanel and overlays untested under Stage Manager; matters for W2.
