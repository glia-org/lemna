What the films' code sounds like, and what may be used

From interviews with Dane A. Davis, the films' supervising sound editor and sound designer, and a review of the program he named (film §1). No source gives a tempo or a register (film §3).

"Electrical and wet at the same time."The directors' brief for the code, as Davis told it; elsewhere relayed as electric and at once biological, "like a living organism" (film takeaway 1; the Academy page itself refused to load).
About 70 % MetaSynth.The code was mostly made in MetaSynth 1.0, for its "sweet, natural rendering"; its image synth plays a picture as oscillators, height = pitch, brightness = level, colour = pan: clean tones at scattered pitches (film takeaway 2, 4).
Water dripping into a barrel.Synthetic sounds layered with drips, then edited to remove obvious frequencies: the wet half, and no pitch that stands out (film takeaway 3).
Dense, not sparse.Sparse separate tones are the changing-state sound that adds 4–7 points of serial-recall errors whatever the level (sound §1.4). The sketch's "sparse glass ticks" fail the rain's steadiness test; heard in CB3's card (film §4).
Not the films' audio, not their name.No rip, no resynthesis of one; Freesound uploads titled after the film were not used. The bed is "Code bed", as the ambience is Code (DESIGN A1, pass 25).
No CC0 "digital rain" that is also steady.21 Freesound sounds measured: the on-topic ones are bursty, clicky, pulsed or mostly hiss; two came close enough to show (candidates §4).
Kept, under every pick.The Rain bed and Still's preset; BD1's rules for any bed: −43 LUFS (Bed.level), in over 1.5 s and out over 0.6 s, only while a container is up and writing goes on, silenced by ⌘K, only the chosen bed in memory (90 s is 35 MB as floats), off on a first launch (DESIGN K1, BD1; LemnaClicks README).
What any CB adds.A second entry in BedChoice.beds, ("Code bed", "Code"); a folder Beds/Code with its CAF and bed.json (lufs as candidates §1); its lines in THIRD_PARTY.md and build_packs.sh; CB3 or CB4 bring synth_code.py into lib/LemnaClicks/tools. About 1–1.5 MB in the app.
Amended by these picks.D1 amends TH1's Lemna preset ("the Keychron pack, no bed, Ambience Off, the first launch's"). CB1 adds a CC BY credit line, as the Electric pack has.
Not on this panel, for later.A bed that follows the ambience (Ambience ▸ Code switching the bed): a new coupling nobody asked for; two beds layered at run time (the engine plays one; CB4 is pre-mixed instead); a bed level control (sound §2.3).

CB: the Code bed's sound

Each a seamless loop, 48 kHz stereo, AAC 128 kbit/s in CAF like the Rain bed, at −26 LUFS in the file and −43 when played. The spectrograms show 20 s, 100 Hz at the bottom to 16 kHz at the top on a log scale, brighter louder. Play one, then type below.

For comparison: the Rain bed today · felix.blume 585845, CC0

90 s of night rain in a forest, BD1's pick (sound §3.1). The bar every Code bed is measured against.

Type here: the Keychron clicks at volume 0.6

Letters, Space, Return and Delete play the default pack's samples with its ±3 % rate, as the app does; the bed and the clicks then stand as they would in Lemna, 12 LU apart.

CB1

MetaSynth trill

A dense trill of synthesised chirps between 2 and 8 kHz, made in MetaSynth, the program most of the films' code came from.

Fishdog, "metasynth bugnite.aif", Freesound 41497, CC BY 3.0 · 2007 · a recorded voice turned into "a swampy backdrop" by MetaSynth · 70 s from 3.6 s, wrap matched to 0.19 dB (candidates §1)

  • The films' own tool: tiny clean tones, rendered as MetaSynth renders a picture (film takeaway 2).
  • The steadiest measured: 3 s loudness SD 0.34 LU (rain 0.49), no bursts, no onsets; the clicks stand 22.1 dB over it, 19.1 dB in 1–4 kHz (rain 21.3, 20.1) (candidates §3).
  • Nothing under 1.8 kHz, so no body, and mono: it sits in the middle of the head.

Risk. A faint swell every 0.6–0.7 s (autocorrelation 0.20; the rain's 0.05), a lilt an ear can lock onto; it reads as insects at night as much as code (its author: "birds? frogs? bugs?"); its source voice is not named; a credit line in THIRD_PARTY and on the site.

CB2

Granular sand and rain

"Rain and sand hitting a metallic plate", made from vinyl crackle, modulated noise and bit reduction.

bassimat, "Sand and Rain Texture from Granular Synthesis", Freesound 838983, CC0 · 2025 · sources named as the author's own · 63 s from 1.7 s, wrap 0.41 dB (candidates §1)

  • CC0, wide, and the clicks stand out most over it: 23.1 dB, 24.8 in 1–4 kHz.
  • Bit-reduced and rain-like: digital and wet, in one recording.

No: the least steady. 3 s loudness SD 1.13 LU and loudness range 3.3 LU, twice the rain's; it swells and nearly drops out in 200–800 Hz over seconds, and with a 63 s loop a swell comes back every minute; 15 crackles a minute (candidates §3).

CB3

Synthesised code

recommended

Clean tone grains that rise like drops, in a short hollow room, over a faint electrical hum: the films' recipe, dense enough to fuse into a trickle.

Synthesised for Lemna, synth_code.py, seed 1999 · no third-party material · 90 s, periodic by construction (candidates §2)

  • Each layer from the makers' account (film §2): MetaSynth's pixels → 60 sine grains a second, 2.2–6.5 kHz, at random pans; water into a barrel → each grain rising 5–25 % as a drop does, a 0.7 s room; electricity → a 55 Hz hum 17 dB down; obvious frequencies removed → no pitch held.
  • As steady as the rain: 3 s loudness SD 0.50 LU, no bursts, no onsets, no period (0.04); the clicks stand 23.0 dB over it, 20.3 in 1–4 kHz, and it leaves 0.7–2 kHz empty, where the clicks' body is (candidates §3).
  • No seam, no licence question, tunable: every grain, the room's tail and the noise are wrapped round exactly 90 s; density, pitch range, hum and seed are parameters, so a second round after listening costs a re-render.

Risk. Never heard: synthetic grains can sound plasticky and a hum like a fault (it can go). Dense grains fuse; sparse ones are the changing-state sound. The sketch's sparse ticks, 6 a second, for comparison: 190 separately heard grains a minute, bursts 4.6 %, 3 s loudness SD 1.83 LU.

CB3 sparse, not an option: what the brief's sparse ticks would be.

CB4

Rain under the code

The Rain bed with CB3's grains over it at the same loudness: synthetic sound laid over water, as the films' code was made.

felix.blume 585845 (CC0, the Rain bed's loop) + synth_code.py · both on the same 90 s circle (candidates §1)

  • The most literal "electrical and wet": Davis's layering of synthetic sound over drips (film takeaway 3).
  • The steadiest of all: 3 s loudness SD 0.35 LU, fluctuation as the rain's, nothing tonal; the clicks stand 21.8 dB over it, 20.2 in 1–4 kHz.

Risk. Two of the three choices in Sound ▸ would be rain, where the ask is the films' sound "additionally to the real rain"; a second 1.5 MB file of mostly the same rain. The fallback if CB3 alone sounds too synthetic.

How to read it. The bar is the rain's (sound §1.5): 3 s loudness SD at most about 1 LU, no bursts (400 ms windows 6 LU over the median), little under 150 Hz, no period (pulse: the envelope's autocorrelation at 0.15–4 s, 0 none, 1 strict). "Clicks over it": the loudest tenth of a 60 s Keychron typing stream at 5 keys a second over the bed at −43 LUFS, median, broadband and in the clicks' 1–4 kHz. Red marks a miss. Previews: the same loops as m4a; the browser's loop may leave a short gap at the wrap that the app's sample-exact wrap does not have.

The Code bed in Sound ▸, and choosing Theme ▸ Lemna turns it on.

  • Pairs the sound with the picture for whoever chooses Lemna, as Still pairs rain with still water.
  • A first launch is unchanged: a preset is written only when the theme is chosen (chooseTheme), so BD1's "off by default" holds.

Ask: against TH1 as it stands. Lemna's preset is "the Keychron pack, no bed, Ambience Off, the first launch's" (Theme.swift). Its ambience is Off, so "the bed when Code is the ambience" has nothing to attach to: D1 turns the bed on alone, or must also set Ambience ▸ Code, which changes what choosing Lemna does to the picture. And the default is where the evidence matters: the Code bed is the less proven sound (synthetic tones, no study), sound §1.5's rule for a bed is "opt-in and off by default".
