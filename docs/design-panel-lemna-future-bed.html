Lemna future bed design options

The user, 2026-11-02, having heard the Code bed candidates: “Do not apply the new sounds, I don't like them.” Then: “To have an alternative for the matrix ambient sound, maybe look for cool programming/future vibe sounds that work as background sounds and that are freely available and that we could add based on their license. Show popular ones when you are unsure.”

One decision: FB, which sound becomes a second bed beside the Rain bed in Session › Sound ▸, or two of them, or none. No tone grains and nothing synthesised for Lemna this time: every candidate is a recording or a designed ambience of a machine, a ship or space, or (one) a synth pad, CC0 or CC BY 4.0 on Freesound. Five are popular by Freesound's own counts (3.7K–11.8K downloads); three of those fail the steadiness rule and are here because you asked to hear popular ones. Everything comes from research_notes/Lemna future bed/candidates.md, cited as candidates with its sections; sound is research_notes/Lemna themes/sound.md. recommended is the proposed pick, risk a pick with a measured drawback, no advised against by the evidence, popular many downloads and ratings.

Each player starts at Lemna's level for a bed, −43 LUFS, 12 LU under the clicks; its slider can raise it. Type into the field in section 1 to hear the Keychron clicks over whichever bed plays. Nothing here was listened to while it was made: the numbers say steady or not, the ear says ship or fridge.

What was looked for and what was found

Freesound searched in 56 queries sorted by downloads and 6 sorted by rating, CC0 and CC BY only; 43 of its sounds and one OpenGameArt track fetched and measured against the rain's steadiness rule (candidates §1, §4).

Popular is not steady.Of the ten most downloaded Freesound sounds measured (6.2K–27.5K downloads), one passes the rule, FB3; the others swell, blip, pass by or play chords. Popularity here measures how well a sound works in a film or a game, for seconds.
The steady ones are hums.Server rooms, ship cabins, machine halls: machines that run. “Hacker”, “terminal room” and “retro computer” found music, typing (which would sit on the clicks) or a computer booting.
And they are dark.Ship and hall ambiences keep 40–80 % of their energy under 150 Hz, the rain 0.2 %. On laptop speakers they lose 3–8 dB and sound thinner than in headphones; the server room loses 1.3 dB, the rain nothing (candidates §3).
Clicks over a hum.Their tick, in 1–4 kHz, stands clearer over every candidate than over the rain (23.7–48.9 dB, rain 20.1). Their body, 200 Hz–1 kHz, is where they differ: 7.6 dB over FB2, 0.0 over FB1's fans (rain 6.0).
Only Freesound gave usable licences.Pixabay's licence forbids distributing a sound “in substantially the same form” and its site refuses scripts; freepd.com has closed; OpenGameArt's popular space entries are music; archive.org's licence search failed (candidates §1.2).
Not the last round.No tone grains, no MetaSynth texture, nothing made for Lemna. The two nearest to that sound are marked: FB6's blips and FB4's held hum.
Kept, under every pick.The Rain bed and Still's preset; BD1's rules for any bed: −43 LUFS (Bed.level), in over 1.5 s and out over 0.6 s, only while a container is up, silenced by ⌘K, only the chosen bed in memory, off on a first launch (DESIGN K1, BD1; LemnaClicks README).
What a pick adds.A folder Beds/<Name> with its CAF (1.2–1.5 MB) and bed.json (lufs as candidates §2); one more entry in Session › Sound ▸ beside the Rain bed; its lines in THIRD_PARTY.md and build_packs.sh; for FB5 and FB6 a credit line. Two picks are two menu items: the engine plays one bed at a time. The card titles are placeholders for the menu names.
Not on this panel.A theme that turns the new bed on (the Code bed's D2: none does); the rain under a hum (CB4's idea, pre-mixed; nobody asked); a level per bed (sound §2.3).

FB: the second bed's sound

Each a seamless 90 s loop, 48 kHz stereo, AAC 128 kbit/s in CAF like the Rain bed, at −26 LUFS in the file and −43 when played. The spectrograms show 20 s, 40 Hz at the bottom to 16 kHz at the top on a log scale, brighter louder. Play one, then type below.

For comparison: the Rain bed today · felix.blume 585845, CC0

90 s of night rain in a forest, BD1's pick (sound §3.1). The bar every candidate is measured against.

Type here: the Keychron clicks at volume 0.6

Letters, Space, Return and Delete play the default pack's samples with its ±3 % rate, as the app does; the bed and the clicks then stand as they would in Lemna, 12 LU apart.

FB1

Server room

risk: masks the clicks' body

Two server stacks in a small server room: fans and moving air, recorded as they run.

KVV_Audio, "AMBTech_Server Room Noise 01_KVV_FREE", Freesound 715475, CC0 · 2023 · WAV 96/24, an XY pair · 161 downloads, too few ratings to show · 90 s from 6.2 s, wrap matched to 0.09 dB

  • The most “programming” of the set: a real server room, nothing designed (candidates §2).
  • The steadiest measured: 3 s loudness SD 0.17 LU (rain 0.49), range 0.5 LU, no onsets, no period, so nothing to recognise on the next pass (candidates §3).
  • Holds up on laptop speakers: 1.3 dB lost under 200 Hz (the hums lose 3–8).

Risk: the clicks lose their thock. Its fan noise fills the clicks' body: in 200 Hz–1 kHz they stand 0.0 dB over it (rain 6.0), so they keep their tick (23.7 dB in 1–4 kHz) and lose their weight. Fan noise is also the white and pink noise of D1's own evidence (g −0.21 without ADHD; sound §1.1). Few downloads.

FB2

Ship cabin

recommendedpopular

A dark, even hull hum, as from inside a ship in flight; nothing above 1 kHz.

jaava73, "inside spaceship.wav", Freesound 45347, CC0 · 2007 · WAV 44.1/16 · 3.7K downloads, 4.3 from 71 ratings · 90 s from 5.3 s, wrap 0.11 dB

  • Popular and CC0: no credit line; the 15th most downloaded of the 43 Freesound sounds measured, and the second most downloaded that passes, after FB3.
  • As steady as the rain: 3 s loudness SD 0.36 LU, range 1.0 LU, no onsets, no period, nothing tonal (candidates §3).
  • The clicks stand clearer over it than over the rain in every band: 20.2 dB broadband, 48.9 in 1–4 kHz, 7.6 in their body at 200 Hz–1 kHz (rain 21.3, 20.1, 6.0). [I] The hull around the screens the code falls on.

Risk. Dark: half its energy under 150 Hz, so laptop speakers drop 3.2 dB of it and it sounds thinner there than in headphones. Its author names no source: it could be any machine, or a fridge to some ears.

FB3

Machine hall

second pickpopular

Soft ventilation over a hollow rumble in a large hall: a reactor room, a server hall.

Kinoton, "Room Tone, Sci Fi, Large Hall", Freesound 353159, CC0 · 2016 · WAV 48/24 · 7.0K downloads, 4.7 from 103 ratings · 90 s from 28.9 s, wrap 0.17 dB

  • The most downloaded candidate that is steady by the rule; CC0. Its author: “Possible applications: Space Ship, Server Room, Military Lab, Reactor Room”.
  • The widest: its channels correlate at 0.05 (rain 0.22), so it sits around the head, not in it.
  • Clicks clear: 18.3 dB broadband, 44.5 in 1–4 kHz, 4.4 in their body.

Risk. At the rule's edge: 3 s loudness SD 0.94 LU (twice the rain's), range 2.3 LU, a slow wander in 150–500 Hz over about a second; darker than FB2 (4.9 dB lost on laptop speakers). The Code round set it aside: “A rumble …; a possible low layer, not needed”.

FB4

Station drone

risk: a 3 s throb

The hum and buzz of a space station's machinery: one held chord of hum harmonics.

db3005, "Space Station Drone", Freesound 686237, CC0 · 2023 · WAV 48/32 · 844 downloads, 5.0 from 32 ratings · 90 s from 20.3 s, wrap 0.42 dB

  • Electrical: the films' future scenes ran on electricity (a Jacob's ladder for the ship's engine; code bed film §1).
  • Steady in loudness: 0.24 LU, range 0.7; its 69 tonal peaks are one held harmonic series, continuous pitch, not a tune (sound §1.4).

Risk: a lilt. A throb every 3 s (its envelope correlates at 0.29 at 3 s in 150–500 Hz; pulse 0.22 at 3.03 s, the rain's 0.05) and slow 9 dB sweeps in 1–4 kHz: a regular movement an ear can lock onto, the risk CB1 had. Dark: 5.5 dB lost on laptop speakers.

FB5

Neon rain

risk: rain again

Generative rain over a distant city hum, made in a synthesiser: the neon city's version of the Rain bed.

Rmshh, "Cyberpunk rain ambience (rain and city noises stem)", Freesound 743999, CC BY 4.0 · 2024 · 234 downloads, 4.8 from 4 ratings · 90 s from 16.2 s, wrap 0.18 dB

  • Steady: 0.62 LU, range 1.9 LU, no onsets, no period, wide (channels uncorrelated).
  • Holds up on laptop speakers: 1.6 dB lost.

Risk: rain again. Two of three beds would be rain, as CB4 was. It fills the clicks' body (1.7 dB over it in 200 Hz–1 kHz, rain 6.0) and swells slowly in 1–4 kHz. A credit line in THIRD_PARTY and, before Lemna goes to others, where a user sees it. Few downloads.

FB6

Sci-fi lab

no: blipspopular

A starship research laboratory: a rumble, air ducts and pseudo-random blips.

qubodup, "Sci-fi Laboratory Ambience", Freesound 212025, CC BY 4.0 (the page’s field; its text says 3.0) · 2013 · Ogg at source, mixed from three CC0 sounds · 9.7K downloads, 4.8 from 102 ratings · 90 s from 48.7 s, wrap 0.27 dB

  • The most downloaded sci-fi interior found: “a computer science center” in its author’s words.
  • Its body is steady (0.27 LU) and leaves the clicks clear (17.1 / 39.5 / 9.6 dB).

No: changing state. 132 blips a minute: sparse tonal events, the sound that disrupts verbal memory whatever the level (sound §1.4), and the nearest thing on this panel to the tone grains of the last round. On laptop speakers 7.8 dB of the rumble goes and the blips are most of what is left. A credit line.

FB7

Deep space

no: swellspopular

A breath blown into a microphone, slowed and pitched down: space, by sound design.

Sonicfreak, "Space Ambience.wav", Freesound 174450, CC0 · 2013 · WAV 48/16 · 11.8K downloads, 4.8 from 108 ratings, the most downloaded CC0 space ambience found · 90 s from 4.3 s, wrap 0.47 dB

  • The most popular CC0 sound of this search; no credit.

No: it swells. 3 s loudness SD 1.73 LU, loudness range 4.9 LU (rain 0.49, 1.1): it rises and falls like breathing, and in a 90 s loop each swell comes back every 90 s, a landmark (sound §2.2).

FB8

Synth pad

no: chordspopular

An ambient synth pad: a slow chord progression in F♯ over a drone, at 120 bpm.

PhonZz, "Angelic Pad Loop.wav", Freesound 242773, CC0 · 2014 · WAV 44.1/16 · 6.5K downloads, 4.7 from 214 ratings, the most rated of the set · 90 s from 90.8 s, wrap 0.25 dB

  • The “lo-fi electronic pad” of the brief: music, not a place.
  • Holds up on laptop speakers: 0.8 dB lost.

No: a tune. A chord progression is segmented pitch change, the case sound §1.4 rules out (27 tonal peaks, range 3.0 LU); it fills the clicks' body (−0.8 dB). A 90 s cut of a 64-bar progression also wraps mid-phrase.

How to read it. The bar is the rain's (sound §1.5): 3 s loudness SD at most about 1 LU, no bursts (none here has any), no period (pulse: the envelope's autocorrelation at 0.15–4 s, 0 none, 1 strict), no events. “Clicks over it”: the loudest tenth of a 60 s Keychron typing stream at 5 keys a second over the bed at −43 LUFS, median, broadband, in the clicks' 1–4 kHz tick and in their 200 Hz–1 kHz body. “Laptop”: the loudness lost under a 200 Hz high-pass, a stand-in for laptop speakers. Red marks a miss of the rule, a body margin under 2 dB or more than 5 dB lost on a laptop. Previews: the same loops as m4a; the browser's loop may leave a short gap at the wrap that the app's sample-exact wrap does not have.
