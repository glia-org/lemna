Recommendation

J2 for the icon; Q2 for the mark, taken.

  1. J2. The fade ties the two picks together: M3's line, ending as I1's text does, fading in toward the caret. At 70 % the mark keeps round 1's margins; the fade costs nothing at small sizes, where J1 and J2 are all but the same pixels.
  2. Q2, taken. On a Retina menu bar the user sees the M3 that was picked; a 1× screen gets a whole row of pixels instead of a wisp.
  3. J1 if the fade is not wanted; J3 if the icon should read larger in lists, at the cost of a crowded square at 256 pt.

How it is built

2026-10-28, on this Mac (macOS 26). Not yet seen in the real menu bar, Finder or System Settings.

Pipelineswift make-icon.swift <icon.svg> build: NSImage reads the SVG and draws it at each of iconutil's ten sizes (16 to 1024 px) into an sRGB bitmap, the shadow under one transparency layer (on the 1024 canvas 10 px down, 24 px blur, 35 % black: AppKit's SVG reader skips filters); iconutil -c icns packs build/AppIcon.icns. make-app.sh runs it, copies the .icns into Contents/Resources and sets CFBundleIconFile to AppIcon. Command Line Tools only; two runs give the same bytes.
macOS 26Round 1 asked whether Tahoe would put the icon into a grey tile. It does not: IconServices draws it in its own body, and adds its own glass, a lit rim and a sheen where the caret crosses the line, plainest on dark.
SwitchingThe variant is one line in make-app.sh (icon=../assets/logo/icon-I1-M3-b.svg); assets/logo/icons.py writes the three SVGs from their numbers.
