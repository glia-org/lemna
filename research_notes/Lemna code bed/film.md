# The sound of the films' code rain, as of 2026-11-02

Scope: what the sound under the digital rain of *The Matrix* (1999) and its sequels is made of, so that Lemna's Code
ambience can have a bed that evokes it (the user, 2026-11-02: "Also add the matrix rain sounds from the movie as ambient
noise additionally to the real rain/storm sounds"). The candidates and their measurements are in `candidates.md`; the
panel is `docs/design-panel-lemna-code-bed.html`.

Tags as in `research_notes/Lemna themes/sound.md`: **[P]** practice, interview or review; **[M]** my measurement;
**[I]** inference. **UNVERIFIED** marks what I could not read myself. Pages were read on 2026-11-02.

## The films' audio cannot be used

- The films' soundtracks are copyrighted. No part of them may be sampled, ripped, resynthesised from a rip or
  "cleaned up" into a bed, and none was downloaded here. Fan uploads titled after the film were not used: every
  candidate is either a recording or synthesis whose author states its own sources, or synthesised for Lemna.
- The film's title is a trademark. Lemna names the ambience and the bed **Code** (DESIGN A1, pass 25: "digital rain or
  code rain"); the film's name belongs in research notes, not in the app, the site or `bed.json`.
- Nothing from the films was listened to for this note: what follows is what the people who made the sound said, plus
  what the tool they named does. Tempo and register are not documented anywhere I could read (§3).

## Takeaway

1. **The brief was a contradiction on purpose.** The directors wanted the code to sound "electrical and wet at the same
   time" (Dane A. Davis, the films' supervising sound editor and sound designer; Academy feature on *Resurrections*,
   UNVERIFIED, page refused) and, as relayed elsewhere, electric and at the same time biological, "like a living
   organism" [P].
2. **About 70 % of the code is MetaSynth.** Davis: the Code "was 70% made with that MetaSynth program", version 1.0,
   chosen for its "very sweet, natural rendering quality"; the same textures were the "timbral skeleton" of the film's
   digital world and were re-rendered for *Resurrections* (A Sound Effect, 2022) [P]. In 1999 he used it on "anything
   that had to feel digital", for an "extremely clean and distinct timbre" (filmsound.org, via Designing Sound) [P].
3. **The wet half is water dripping into a barrel.** Davis "layered synthetic sounds with water dripping into a barrel",
   then edited the result digitally to remove obvious frequencies (MPSE event, 2004) [P]. Mix in 1999 called it
   "dripping computer code" [P].
4. **[I] So the sound is a cloud, not a tune.** MetaSynth's image synth plays a picture left to right, each pixel an
   oscillator, height its pitch, brightness its level, colour its pan (Sound On Sound) [P]: a field of glyphs rendered
   that way is many short pure tones at scattered pitches across the stereo field; drips add short rising "plinks"
   with a hollow resonance; removing the obvious frequencies leaves no pitch that stands out.
5. **For Lemna the film is a palette, not a template.** Its code sound is foreground sound design for a few seconds on
   screen; a writing bed has to run 10–20 minutes under the clicks without being noticed (`sound.md` §1.5). The parts
   that carry over: many tiny clean tones (the electrical), drips in a short space (the wet), no pitch that stands out.
   What must not carry over: sparse, separately heard tones, which are the changing-state sound that disrupts verbal
   memory (`sound.md` §1.4: +4–7 points of serial-recall errors, whatever the level).

## 2. The components, as a sound designer would build them

| Component | Evidence | Character [I] | For a writing bed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clean tone grains** (the electrical) | MetaSynth for ~70 % of the code; pixels as oscillators [P] | Very short sine partials at scattered pitches, panned across the field; no grit, no distortion | Keep, but dense: many per second, so they fuse into a trickle instead of being heard one by one (§4) |
| **Drips** (the wet) | Water dripping into a barrel, layered [P] | Short "plinks" that rise in pitch as a bubble's resonance does, a few ms to tens of ms | Keep as a rising chirp on each grain, or as a real rain layer under the grains |
| **A hollow space** | The barrel [P] | A short, dark-edged reverberation that smears the grains | Keep: a short room (≈ 0.7 s) blurs onsets and lowers their salience |
| **No standing pitch** | "remove obvious frequencies" [P] | Nothing tonal that lasts; no tune | Keep: no melody, no chord changes |
| **Electricity** | The film's motif in general, not said of the code [P] | Hum, buzz, sparks | Optional and quiet: a static hum is continuous pitch, which does not disrupt as segmented pitch does (`sound.md` §1.4), but some hear a hum as a fault |
| **A lower code** | *Resurrections*' "grandparent" code, deeper and clunkier (UNVERIFIED) | A low register exists in the family | Not needed under writing; low sound also vanishes on laptop speakers |

## 3. Tempo and register: not documented

No source gives a rate, a pitch range or a level for the code's sound, and none of it was listened to here. What can be
said without the film:
- [I] MetaSynth renders a picture's pixels as simultaneous partials; a frame full of glyphs gives many grains at once,
  not a beat. Nothing in the method imposes a tempo.
- [P] Lemna's own Code draws each column at a constant speed of 8–16 rows a second (DESIGN A1, pass 25, from the
  title sequence's frames); a grain per glyph change would be dozens a second across the screen, again a texture and
  not a pulse.
- [I] Drips into a barrel sit roughly in 0.5–4 kHz (bubble resonances of drops); "glassy" and "twinkling" point higher,
  2–8 kHz. Lemna's clicks centre on 1.9–2.8 kHz (`sound.md` §2.3), so the bed's grains should sit mostly above 2 kHz
  and its body away from 1–4 kHz where it can.

## 4. What this means for a Code bed

- **Dense, not sparse.** The coordinator's sketch was "sparse filtered high glass ticks at random intervals". Sparse
  separate tones are the riskiest sound under writing: tone sequences add 4–7 points of serial-recall errors and level
  does not reduce it (`sound.md` Takeaway 2, §1.4, Ellermeier & Zimmer 2014). The synthesised candidate is therefore
  dense (60 grains a second through a short room), and a sparse render (6 a second) is on the panel only to be heard
  and compared. [M] The sparse render fails the steadiness test that the rain passes (`candidates.md`).
- **Steady like the rain.** The same bar as the rain bed (`sound.md` §1.5): 3 s loudness SD ≤ about 1 LU, no bursts,
  no audible period, little under 150 Hz, well under the clicks, opt-in, stopped with ⌘K.
- **No clicks in the bed.** A texture of sharp broadband clicks would blur the key clicks it sits under; one Freesound
  candidate was rejected for exactly that (`candidates.md`, 869343).
- **Loopable by construction.** A synthesised bed can be built on a circle of exactly 90 s (every grain, the room's
  tail and the noise wrapped round), so it needs no crossfade and can be tuned and re-rendered later.
