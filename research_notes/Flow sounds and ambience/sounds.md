# Key sounds and ambience for Flow, as of 2026-10-28

Scope: which per-keystroke sound families suit a calm one-line writing box at 10–14 keys/s, where permissively licensed samples for each family are, how the established keyboard-sound apps handle variation, key-up and music, and whether an optional ambient bed is worth it. Flow today: one CC0 pack (Keychron K10 linear, Sadiquecat on Freesound, 12 cuts from the lossy previews, 102 KB), key-down only, random pick without immediate repeat, ±3 % playback rate, AVAudioEngine, kinds `letter`, `space`, `paragraph`, `backspace` (`lib/FlowClicks/THIRD_PARTY.md`, `Picker.swift`, `DESIGN.md` K1 and Open 1).

Tags: **[E]** controlled study; **[P]** practice, vendor claim, issue tracker or anecdote; **[M]** my own measurement (method in §4); **[I]** inference from the above. **UNVERIFIED** marks what I could not check. Freesound licences were read on each sound's page on 2026-10-28; sound IDs and uploader handles are given because CC-BY needs them and CC0 uploaders ask for them.

## Takeaway

1. **The earlier finding "no permissively licensed typewriter pack" (DESIGN Open 1) is wrong.** Freesound has CC0 typewriter recordings with separate files per key class: *Silver Typewriter Pack* by moodyfingers (keystroke, space bar, shift ×2, margin release, carriage-return lever ×3, margin release + carriage return; FLAC 44.1 kHz/24-bit, gated, clean), Nox_Sound's 6 keystrokes + Return (48/24 mono, NTG4+), yottasounds' 3 single strikes (96/32), knufds' margin bell + carriage return, _stubb's bell (48/24). A CC-BY 4.0 pack (magedu, 96/24 mono, Rode NTG-3) has both a **manual** machine (typing, space bar ×1 and several, carriage + bell, platen) and an **electric** one (typing ×2, space bar, Return, power on/off). Long clean CC0 runs from which many takes can be cut: megashroom (Smith-Corona Corsair, 96/24, floor −81 dBFS), Garuda1982 (96/24, MixPre-6 + ME66, floor −82), nvmbky (48/24, floor −90). An IBM Selectric II run (secretmojo, CC0, FLAC 48/24) exists but carries motor hum.
2. **Ranked next packs:** (a) *Typewriter, manual*: moodyfingers Silver Typewriter as the one machine (letters, space, Return), its keystroke file alone holds ≈ 14–17 strikes; Nox_Sound / nvmbky takes only if they match by ear; bell from _stubb; all CC0. (b) *Laptop, soft*: Sadiquecat's MacBook Air M2 single keys (CC0, 96/24, same uploader and recorder as today's pack). (c) *Typewriter, electric*: magedu's electric set (CC-BY 4.0, attribution line in THIRD_PARTY.md). (d) Zero-byte *soft tick*: synthesised in the mixer (a short noise burst into one or two resonators), as the MIT web typewriter *platen* does for a whole machine. Everything else is weaker or blocked by licence (§3.9).
3. **Families:** short, dark, low-mid strikes survive 10–14 keys/s best (linear/tactile switches, laptop keys, a soft synthetic tick). A manual typewriter works if the cuts are shortened and the top end is tamed: its strikes ring 60–190 ms and put 36–81 % of their energy above 5 kHz, against 70–123 ms and ≈0–1 % for the Keychron cuts the user likes [M]. Musical notes and water drops are the riskiest for a writing tool: a sequence of changing pitches is the "changing-state" sound that disrupts verbal short-term memory more than a repeated one [E]. Pencil and fountain pen are friction sounds, not impacts; per key they read as tapping, not writing.
4. **Variation:** Flow's no-repeat random pick with ±3 % rate is already gentler than the others: Thock's smallest pitch step is ±2.5 semitones (≈ ±15 %) with repeats allowed; Mechvibes fixes one sample per key at load and has no pitch variation; kbsim's five "random" files are really per-row samples [P]. What a typewriter pack adds: real per-class samples (space has no typebar strike, Backspace is a lighter escapement clack, Return is a 0.3–1 s carriage return), and a heavier strike for capitals instead of a separate Shift sound.
5. **Key-up:** skip for typewriters. A manual typewriter's typebar falls back onto felt meant to be silent; the "key-up" in Mechvibes-family packs is usually the press recording cut in half [P]. With an average key hold of 116 ms [E] and 71–100 ms between keys at speed, key-up sounds land between the next presses and double the event rate [I]. Worth it only for real switch recordings with real release takes, quiet.
6. **Music:** none of Thock, Klack, Mechvibes ducks other audio; macOS has no public ducking API for this case (AVAudioSession is not on macOS) [P]. A volume control relative to the click level, peaks kept at −6 dBFS, the output kept running while the box is up, and a darker timbre are what can be done.
7. **Ambience: lean no** (§8). No study shows a bed helps a typical writer; plain noise slightly harms people without ADHD on average (meta-analysis g = −0.21, +0.25 with ADHD); intelligible speech lowers writing fluency, so no café. macOS already ships rain and noise as Background Sounds (Accessibility › Audio). If built: procedural brown/pink noise, 0 bytes, < 0.04 % CPU, off by default, one level. A recorded 60–120 s CC0 rain loop costs 0.7–1.9 MB as AAC, i.e. about doubles the app.

## 1. What 10–14 keys/s does to a click

- [E] 168,960 online typists: mean 51.56 WPM (SD 20.2), the fastest 120 WPM or more; keypress duration ≈ 116 ms on average and similar across fast and slow typists; rollover (next key down before the last is up) 25 % of keystrokes on average (SD 17 %), 40–70 % among fast typists. Earlier typewriter-era studies cited there: 60–75 WPM, mean inter-key interval 140 ms. — [Dhakal, Feit, Kristensson & Oulasvirta, CHI 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174220) ([PDF](https://www.pokristensson.com/pubs/DhakalEtAlCHI2018.pdf))
- [I] 10–14 keys/s is 120–170 WPM: burst speed within words for a fast typist, not an average. Inter-onset 71–100 ms. A click that is still ringing at 80 ms overlaps the next one; voices stack 2–3 deep. Flow's mixer has 16 voices, sized for clicks up to 150 ms (`Mixer.swift`): enough, also for a 0.5–1 s carriage return or bell, which holds one voice for its length (MechvibesDX: 32 voices, oldest evicted with a 10 ms ramp, [engine.rs](https://github.com/hainguyents13/mechvibes-dx/blob/8c7ba68/src/libs/audio/engine.rs#L10-L13)).
- [M] Time for a strike to fall 30 dB below its peak (median per recording, 2 ms RMS frames): unicae membrane keypress 38 ms; typewriters 62–71 ms (Smith-Corona 390343, nvmbky 801121, ColinMWJones 862556, TBM 860909, Nox_Sound 550340), 117–165 ms (moodyfingers space bar and keystroke, Garuda1982), 190 ms (Selectric, in its hum). Today's Keychron cuts are 70–123 ms long by construction (25 ms fade). The real machines were typed at 2.4–5.5 strikes/s in these recordings [M], so no recording shows a typewriter at 12/s.
- [M] Brightness (spectral centroid of the first 40 ms after the loudest peak, share of energy above 5 kHz): two Keychron letter cuts 2.7 and 3.1 kHz with 0–1 %, space 2.3 kHz, Return 2.1 kHz; unicae membrane 4.0 kHz, 2 %; Kenney `tick_001` 7.8 kHz, 44 %; three typewriter sources 6.7–10 kHz with 36–81 %. Median centroid over all strikes in 13 typewriter recordings: 4.9–10.5 kHz (§4). The pack the user likes is dark. [I] A typewriter pack needs a high shelf (≈ −6 dB from 4–5 kHz) or a gentle low-pass to sit at the same comfort; leave the bell bright, it is rare.
- [E] Changing-state effect: "varying tones being more disruptive than repeated tones" for verbal serial recall (Jones & Macken 1993), while white noise produced no disruption compared with silence (Salamé & Baddeley 1989), both as cited in [Kattner & Meinhardt 2020, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00346/full). Continuous random pitch glides did not disrupt, glides broken regularly by silence did (Jones, Macken & Murray 1993, Memory & Cognition; seen only in a search summary, UNVERIFIED): segmented sounds such as clicks are the disruptive kind when they change. [I] Writing leans on verbal working memory; keystroke sounds whose pitch jumps audibly from key to key (notes, drops, wide random pitch) are the changing-state case. ±3 % (about half a semitone) keeps the stream near steady-state; Thock's ±2.5–10 semitones would not.
- [E] Self-generated sounds evoke smaller auditory-cortex responses than the same sounds played externally (Martikainen et al. 2005, MEG; Schafer & Marcus 1973); the attenuation survives unpredictable frequency or onset but is smaller when frequency is unpredictable (Bäß, Jacobsen & Schröger 2008). Contested: sounds merely coinciding with presses are attenuated too (Horváth et al. 2012). — [Bäß et al. 2008 abstract](https://home.uni-leipzig.de/biocog/archived-20210701/index.php@page=abstract&signature=baess_p2008intjpsychophysiol70_137.html); [Frontiers review 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00041/full). [I] A click that follows the key at once and keeps one timbre is the kind the brain predicts and turns down; latency (K1's budget, under ~20–30 ms) and small variation both serve this.
- From the earlier notes (`Flow writing tool design options/flow_psychology.md` §5): no controlled study of synthetic clicks on a physical keyboard; sound intensity did not affect performance on flat keyboards; feedback is preferred to none.

## 2. Sound families

| Family | What makes it work at speed | What makes it fail | Per-key variation needed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanical, linear / lubed ("thocky")** | Short (≤ 100 ms), dark, low-mid "thock"; one event per key; the user's current pick | Unlubed linears ping (spring); thin if cut too short | 6+ letter takes, own space and Return (stabilised keys sound deeper and rattle), Backspace from its own key if there is a take; capitals no different | Keep as default |
| **Mechanical, tactile (MX Brown, Clear, Topre)** | Like linear plus a soft bump; Topre is cushioned and dark | Hardly distinct from linear once cut to the press | Same as linear | Optional second switch pack; CC0 sources mostly MP3 at source (§3.3) |
| **Mechanical, clicky (MX Blue, buckling spring)** | Crisp, unmistakable | Click jacket adds a sharp high transient; buckling spring adds spring ring; at 12/s a rattle; the classic shared-office complaint ([P] Boing Boing thread in `flow_psychology.md`) | Same as linear, plus real release takes if key-up is wanted | Avoid as default; possible opt-in |
| **Laptop / membrane (MacBook scissor, Cherry KC 1000)** | Very short (membrane keypress 38 ms to −30 dB [M]), soft, quiet: least intrusive | On a MacBook it doubles the real key under the fingers; any lag turns that into a flam [I]; can sound like nothing | 6+ letter takes, space (much louder and longer on a MacBook), Return, Delete | Good "soft" pack (Sadiquecat MBA M2, CC0) |
| **Manual typewriter** | The writer's archetype; a strong sense of "laying down" each letter | Long bright ring (62–190 ms, 36–81 % above 5 kHz); at 12/s real machines jam and a sampled one becomes a machine gun; carriage return every paragraph is long (0.3–1 s) | Distinct classes: letter (8+ takes; heavier variant for capitals), space (escapement only, no typebar), Backspace (lighter clack + carriage step), Return (carriage-return lever: ratchet + slide + margin stop), bell (rare: see §5); no key-up | Recommended second pack, cut short (≈ 90–120 ms), high shelf down |
| **Electric typewriter (Selectric, Olympia Report Electric, Sears Electric Twelve)** | Lighter, faster "thunk" of the type element; can be typed fast | Motor hum under everything: a per-key cut carries a slice of hum that gates on and off with each key [M: Selectric floor −55 dBFS, 67 % of its quiet-frame energy below 150 Hz] | Letter, space, Return (motor-driven carriage return); hum removed from the cuts (high-pass ~150 Hz + gate), optionally a separate faint hum loop while the box is up | Third pack, only with clean per-key takes (magedu, CC-BY 4.0) |
| **Pencil on paper** | Quiet, "analogue" | A friction sound, not an impact: one grain per key reads as tapping; continuous scribble masks the key events and says nothing about each key | Would need a granular "scribble" bed whose density follows the typing rate: a different engine | Not as per-key click |
| **Fountain pen** | As pencil, with nib scratch | Scratch is bright and continuous; same problem as pencil | As pencil | No |
| **Piano / marimba / kalimba notes (Typatone)** | Playful; Typatone maps each letter to a note with six moods ([P] [ORF FM4](https://fm4v3.orf.at/stories/1763720/), [Easterseals Tech](https://eastersealstech.com/2020/10/13/typatone-make-music-while-you-write)) | Changing-state sequence by design [E §1]; sustains overlap into chords at 12/s; a melody pulls attention to sound, away from words [I] | Short damped notes (≤ 150 ms), one scale or chord, fixed note per key or per class | A play mode, not for drafting |
| **Water drops** | Soft, organic at slow rates | Each drop is a pitched "bloop" with a resonant sweep; random pitches = changing-state; at 12/s it sounds like boiling [I]; CC0 drops on Freesound are often mouth-made | 6+ takes of similar pitch, darker for space | No |
| **Soft synthetic tick** | Smallest (zero bytes if synthesised), fully controllable (level, brightness, length), no licence; easy to keep dark and short | Sterile, "UI-like"; Kenney's ticks are bright (7.8 kHz centroid) and lossy | Per class: pitch/brightness steps (space lower and longer, Return lowest) | Good fallback and "minimal" option |

Per-key structure in practice:
- [P] Real typewriter anatomy as modelled by *platen* (MIT, procedural Web Audio, created 2026-10-09): key = keytop tick at 0, typebar hits the platen at 12–25 ms, escapement 20–38 ms after the strike, key return 65–115 ms after (16–20 dB below the strike); space = bar bottoms out 8–14 ms, escapement 18–32 ms later, no typebar; Backspace = keytop tick, lever clack 16–24 ms, carriage steps back; Shift = basket clunk; bell = trip click then dome 12–22 ms later; carriage return lasts `0.12 + 0.004 × columns` s (lever, up to four line-space ratchet clicks 34 ms apart, slide, margin-stop thunk). Measured relative levels: escapement 8–14 dB below the strike, key return 16–19.5 dB below; small slugs (`. , '`) 2.5 dB lighter and brighter, wide letters (`m w M W @ # % &`) 0.8 dB heavier, capitals 1–3 dB louder and bassier. 24 strike variants (4 basket zones × 6), each character with a fixed basket position by hash; no immediate repeats; levelling by each variant's loudest 4 ms. At 12 keys/s with spaces it measured peak −5.9 dBFS, median peak-to-valley 44.6 dB (keys stay separate). The author states "Nobody has listened to it": tuned against measurements, not by ear. — [platen README](https://github.com/winchxyz/platen), [dev/AUDIO_NOTES.md](https://github.com/winchxyz/platen/blob/main/dev/AUDIO_NOTES.md), [js/audio.js](https://github.com/winchxyz/platen/blob/main/js/audio.js)
- [P] Tickeys' typewriter scheme (macOS, MIT code, no sound credits): 5 key sounds + space, backspace, return, scroll up/down ([schemes.json](https://github.com/yingDev/Tickeys/blob/da238a9/Tickeys.app/Contents/Resources/data/schemes.json)). Noisy Typer: letters, space, backspace, return, scroll; a user found its stereo spread "totally distracting" ([F.A.T. Lab](https://fffff.at/noisy-typer-a-typewriter-for-your-laptop)). Hanx Writer (iOS, 2014): key taps, space bar, bell at line end, carriage return; each "model" its own sound set; whether recorded from real machines UNVERIFIED ([CreativePro](https://creativepro.com/?p=14728)).
- [P] Emacs `selectric-mode` (LGPL code) uses exactly the CC0 Freesound recordings found here: secretmojo's Selectric II (224012) for typing and carriage, knufds (345955) for the bell ([README](https://github.com/rbanffy/selectric-mode)).

## 3. Where the samples are

All Freesound pages are `https://freesound.org/people/<user>/sounds/<id>/`, packs `https://freesound.org/people/<user>/packs/<id>/`. Originals (WAV/FLAC/AIFF) download only with a Freesound login (or the API with OAuth2); a logged-in user can take a whole pack as one zip at `…/packs/<id>/download/`. Public lossy previews: `https://cdn.freesound.org/previews/<id ÷ 1000>/<id>_<uploader id>-hq.ogg` (or `-hq.mp3`); uploader ids are given where I fetched them. "Gated" = digital silence between hits (cleaned by the uploader). "MP3 at source" = the original upload is lossy, so even the login download is lossy. Format = original's container, rate, bits, channels.

## 5. Per-key mapping for a typewriter pack in Flow

- **letter**: 8–16 takes from one machine; capitals (and the shifted symbols) a heavier variant: +1–2 dB and ≈ −3 % rate, instead of a separate Shift event ([P] platen's capitals 1–3 dB louder and bassier). Optional fixed take per character (hash), as kbsim/Tickeys/Mechvibes do per key, on top of the no-repeat rule.
- **space**: its own takes (no typebar: a dull bar thud and the escapement). moodyfingers 734318, magedu 277299/277298.
- **backspace**: a lighter clack plus a carriage step back. Only khwez 712231 (CC0, MP3 at source) was found; else a letter take at −6 dB, shortened, rate 0.9 (as today's Keychron pack does).
- **paragraph (Return)**: carriage-return lever: ratchet + slide + margin-stop thunk, 0.3–1 s (moodyfingers 734310–734312; Nox_Sound's Return in 550340; CallFlan's line ratchets for a shorter variant). Paragraphs are rare, so a long sound there does not pile up.
- **bell**: Flow has no lines, so a margin bell has no natural trigger. Options: (a) never; (b) once on ⌘⏎ (Done) as "ding + carriage return", the hand-off cue: the earlier notes suggested a bell only at the session's end; (c) every N characters as a fake line end (rejected here: an unasked-for event at a random moment is distraction [I]).
- **caret movement (←/→, ⌥/⌘ variants, B4)**: no sound, or a quiet escapement tick at −12 dB; a typewriter has no caret.
- **key-up**: none.
- **Cut length**: end before the escapement's ring dies (≈ 90–120 ms with a 25 ms fade, as now); a high shelf of ≈ −6 dB from 4–5 kHz; peaks to −6 dBFS like the default pack; normalise each take by its loudest few ms rather than by peak, so one lucky transient does not jump out (platen's levelling).
- **Size**: 16 cuts × ~0.1 s + Return 0.6 s + bell 1 s at 48 kHz/16-bit mono ≈ 150 + 58 + 96 KB ≈ 0.3 MB. Today's pack is 102 KB.

## 7. Clicks and the user's music [P]

- macOS has no AVAudioSession (it exists on iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, tvOS, visionOS, watchOS: [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession)), so no session-level ducking of other apps. `AVAudioInputNode.voiceProcessingOtherAudioDuckingConfiguration` (macOS 14+) ducks others only with voice processing on the microphone input: unfit ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioinputnode/voiceprocessingotheraudioduckingconfiguration)). Core Audio process taps (macOS 14.2+) can mute and re-render another process's audio but need the audio-capture permission (`NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription`) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreaudio/capturing-system-audio-with-core-audio-taps)): far too heavy for a click, and a privacy prompt for a writing box.
- What the apps do: Mechvibes compensates for system volume; Thock has per-device volume, output-device selection and an "Auto-mute on music playback" (off by default) that polls `osascript` every 3 s for Music, Spotify, VLC and mutes fully ([AudioMonitor.swift](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/blob/ec787b1/Thock/Services/AudioMonitor.swift#L56)); Klack caps volume at system volume, offers output selection (1.3) and a "media" sleep trigger (2.1) ([changelog](https://tryklack.com/changelog)); none ducks. A ducking request in Mechvibes was closed without reply ([#29](https://github.com/hainguyents13/mechvibes/issues/29)); clicks too loud when the system volume is raised for media ([thock#138](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/issues/138), [#74](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/issues/74), [mechvibes#326](https://github.com/hainguyents13/mechvibes/issues/326)).
- Bluetooth: Thock's AirPods distortion and glitches came from stopping and restarting the queue after idle (codec re-negotiation); the fix keeps the queue warm and uses a 512-frame buffer on Bluetooth ([thock#113](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/issues/113), [#160](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/issues/160), [PR #107](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/pull/107)). First key after idle late: [mechvibes#13](https://github.com/hainguyents13/mechvibes/issues/13).
- [I] For Flow: keep one toggle (K1) and add at most a click level; keep the engine running while the box is up; a dark, short timbre masks less of the music's high band and is masked less by it; do not try to duck. A separate output device is a non-goal for a one-line box.

## 8. Ambient sound beds

Researched by a second agent (2026-10-28); its sizes and CPU figures were measured on an M2 Pro with `afconvert`/clang, its loop lengths read from the players' public audio files. Its claims spot-checked here: the arithmetic (sizes, the myNoise LCM) holds.

### 8.1 Does it help writing?

Solid:
- [E] Background sound hurts reading: Bayesian meta-analysis of 65 studies, noise, speech and music all "small but reliably detrimental"; intelligible speech and music with lyrics worst. — [Vasilev, Kirkby & Angele 2018](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/)
- [E] Writing: background speech, attended or not, lowered writing fluency but not quality; holding 6 digits cut fluency by nearly half and lowered quality. — [Ransdell, Levy & Kellogg 2002](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/hpd_hs_facarticles/259). Background music reduced fluency (N = 45). — [Ransdell & Gilroy 2001](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/hpd_hs_facarticles/253)
- [E] Story typing: background speech had a small effect; a dialogue disrupted more than one side of a conversation. — [Keus van de Poll & Sörqvist 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5074307). More intelligible speech (higher STI) disrupts writing more — [dissertation summary](https://www.avhandlingar.se/om/keus/) (the threshold STI > 0.23 UNVERIFIED). Three talkers in an open-plan room lowered writing performance — [TU Eindhoven 2020](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/the-influence-of-background-speech-on-a-writing-task-in-an-open-p/)
- [E] Music overall null in a meta-analysis, but reading disturbed and memory slightly harmed. — [Kämpfe et al. 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735610376261)
- [E] Mechanism: a verbal working-memory load interferes with sentence generation (Kellogg, Olive & Piolat 2007, Acta Psychologica 124:382–397; seen only in secondary summaries).

Weak:
- [E] Mehta, Zhu & Cheema 2012: five experiments, one lab; 70 dB ambient noise beat 50 dB on creativity tasks (Remote Associates Test), 85 dB hurt; three levels only; no direct replication found; effect sizes not in the abstract (UNVERIFIED); the stimulus was reportedly a blend of café, restaurant and traffic, so likely with voices. — [J. Consumer Research 39(4)](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/39/4/784/1795620). Coffitivity cites it ([PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-03/replicate-coffeeshop-your-cubicle-more-efficient-work/)).
- [E] More ideas in ambient noise, not more original ones, depending on cognitive flexibility (N = 42). — [Mones & Massonnié 2022](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10155659)
- [E] White/pink noise meta-analysis: ADHD or elevated symptoms g = 0.249 (95 % CI 0.135–0.363; 13 studies, N = 335); **without ADHD g = −0.212 (harm)**; exposures of minutes, almost all white noise, one pink, none brown; six studies share one author. — [Nigg et al. 2024, JAACAP 63(8)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/). Söderlund et al.: white noise helped inattentive children and hurt attentive ones ([2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17683456/); [2010, N = 51, 78 dB](https://behavioralandbrainfunctions.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1744-9081-6-55)).
- [E] Nature sounds: stress/annoyance g = −0.60, health/positive affect g = 1.63 (CI 0.09–3.16) — health outcomes, not writing ([Buxton 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33753555/)); nature sounds *before* tasks improved a digit-span/n-back composite vs urban sounds ([Van Hedger 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30367351/); N UNVERIFIED); office noise 51 vs 39 dB lowered word recall and raised tiredness, river sounds in a break helped motivation (Jahncke 2011, N = 47).
- No study of ambient beds or key clicks in writing apps found.

[I] Net: for a typical writer an ambient bed is at best neutral and noise is slightly harmful on average; anything with voices lowers fluency; the one solid benefit is masking speech in the room, and for people with attention problems a small benefit over minutes. A café bed is out.

### 8.2 How players loop without seams [P, measured by the agent]

- **A Soft Murmur**: main loops rain 120.0 s, wind 120.0 s, waves 130.0 s, fire 58.5 s, thunder 318.2 s; stereo Ogg Vorbis ≈ 190 kbit/s (rain 2.80 MB); every sound has a 10.0 s "glue" clip: when the main loop is within half a glue length of its end the glue starts, and at the glue's midpoint the main loop restarts. "Meander" randomises each sound's volume. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1175522255))
- **myNoise** (rain): 10 octave-band sliders, two stems (A, B) each, 20 Vorbis files, 5.1 MB; band lengths 24, 23, … 15 s; low bands stored at 8 and 32 kHz; player comment: "Each slider owns two stems, A and B, alternating on a fixed period … with B starting half an A-length after A". The whole mix repeats only after LCM(15…24) = 411,863,760 s ≈ 13 years, though each band repeats every 15–24 s. "Animate" moves sliders between 50 % and 125 %. Field recordings, no generative AI. ([rain generator](https://mynoise.net/NoiseMachines/rainNoiseGenerator.php), [help](https://mynoise.net/NoiseMachines/help.php), [FAQ](https://mynoise.net/faq.php))
- **platen** (web, procedural): rain and fireplace 40 s, mantel clock 30 s (exactly 30 periods), summer night 40 s; each bed rendered with an overhang that is equal-power crossfaded into its start, events written modulo the loop length so tails wrap; seam checked against the 99.9th percentile of ordinary sample steps and RMS within 1.2 dB; **no rare events** (thunder, a falling log) on purpose, because they would be recognised on every loop; ambience bypasses the key compressor so typing does not pump the rain; default ambience fader 0.5 (gain = v²). ([AUDIO_NOTES.md](https://github.com/winchxyz/platen/blob/main/dev/AUDIO_NOTES.md))
- Noisli streams HLS (`cdn.noisli.com/hls/<sound>/…m3u8`); loop lengths UNVERIFIED. Coffitivity loops long recordings with "little snippets of conversation"; lengths UNVERIFIED.
- **On AVAudioEngine**: `scheduleBuffer(_:at:options: .loops)` loops a buffer indefinitely ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioplayernodebufferoptions)). AAC carries 2112 priming samples plus remainder padding ([TN2258](https://developer.apple.com/library/mac/technotes/tn2258/_index.html)); an `afconvert` .m4a decoded to exactly its valid frames (measured), so a loop decoded into a buffer is sample-exact. Avoid MP3 for loops (gapless handling UNVERIFIED). Crossfade tail into head with equal-power (sin/cos) ramps: a linear fade dips ≈ 3 dB on uncorrelated noise. One-shots on a second player node at random `AVAudioTime`s.
- **Procedural**: Paul Kellet's pink filters ("refined" ±0.05 dB above 9.2 Hz; "economy" 3 poles ±0.5 dB) and Voss-McCartney ([firstpr.com.au](https://www.firstpr.com.au/dsp/pink-noise/)); brown as a leaky integrator, e.g. `out = (last + 0.02 * white) / 1.02; out *= 3.5` ([react-native-audio-api](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-audio-api/docs/guides/noise-generation)); in an `AVAudioSourceNode` render block (macOS 10.15+, realtime-safe: no allocation) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosourcenoderenderblock)).

### 8.4 Size and CPU

- 16-bit PCM 48 kHz stereo = 192,000 B/s: 60 s = 11.52 MB, 180 s = 34.56 MB; mono half. Measured on 180 s stereo brown noise (a codec's worst case): AAC 128k 2.98 MB (≈ 0.96 MB per 60 s), AAC 64k 1.51 MB, Opus-in-CAF 1.05 MB (asked 64k, got ≈ 46 kbit/s; macOS 10.13+, CBR only). `AVAudioFile` reads them into PCM buffers.
- Preloaded as deinterleaved Float32: 384 kB/s stereo, 60 s = 23 MB RAM, 180 s = 69 MB. Decoding 180 s took 0.10 s (AAC) and 0.36 s (Opus). Streaming saves memory but then the seam is the app's problem.
- Procedural (clang -O2, fast RNG): Kellet refined pink 0.31 s CPU per hour of 48 kHz mono (0.009 % of a core), brown 0.62 s per hour (0.017 %); double for stereo. Zero bytes. Keeping the engine running costs more than the noise (not measured).

### 8.6 Verdict

**Lean no.** No study shows an ambient bed helps a typical writer; noise slightly harms people without ADHD on average (g −0.21); speech in a bed lowers writing fluency; and macOS already ships rain and noise as Background Sounds, which a user can turn on with Flow at zero cost to the app. If it is built anyway, the smallest version: procedural brown or pink noise in an `AVAudioSourceNode` (≈ 40 lines, 0 bytes, < 0.04 % CPU), off by default, one level, ≈ 1 s fades, independent of the clicks, no café. A recorded alternative (one CC0 rain loop of 60–120 s, AAC 96–128k stereo, ≈ 0.7–1.9 MB, crossfade-looped, 23–46 MB RAM decoded) roughly doubles the app for a benefit the evidence does not show.
